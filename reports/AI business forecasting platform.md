# Sell Human-Checked Forecasts First, Platform Later

**Yes. A solo beginner in Egypt working with Claude can build and sell this, but in a narrower and more honest form than the full vision. Start with a human-reviewed, done-for-you "forecast and decision" report for formal SMEs in Egypt and the Gulf, and turn it into a platform only after the same report has been sold and delivered 10–20 times.** The technology is not what holds this back. Free statistical libraries and commercially licensed foundation models such as Amazon's Chronos run on an ordinary CPU, Claude can write most of the code, and the AI cost of one report is a few dollars against a price of $100 or more. The real limits are commercial and about what forecasts can know. Egyptian prices are low: accounting offices charge about EGP 3,000–7,000 a month. Nobody has measured what Egyptian SMEs will pay for analytics. A beginner has no track record. And QuickBooks, Xero, Mastercard and the Gulf's Kudwa (sold inside Wafeq) are already building AI cash-flow forecasting into tools SMEs use. The research also weakens the "AI predicts the future" pitch. Large language models (LLMs) add little to numeric forecasting, and plain statistical ensembles still match or beat newer models on the short, noisy data series that small firms have. What a client will pay for is someone who turns messy books into decisions with dates and amounts attached: when cash runs short and which invoices to chase, whether a price rise survives the sales it loses, whether a March hire is affordable. Each answer is tested under EGP and inflation scenarios, written in Arabic and owned by an accountable person. This research found no competitor that offers that package to Egyptian firms with fewer than about 100 employees. A realistic path is three to five pilots in the next two months, ten paying clients within four to six months through accountant partners, freelance marketplaces and one focused niche, and a platform in roughly 9–18 months. Most figures here come from search-engine extracts of the cited sources, and vendor prices are indicative only, so re-check any number before quoting it to a client.

## Owners pay for cash-runway, pricing and hiring answers

No survey ranks forecast types by how often small firms ask for them, so the ranking has to be read from what owners say hurts. The Federal Reserve's Small Business Credit Survey covered more than 7,600 US employer firms. Their most common financial problems were **rising costs (75%), paying operating expenses (56%), uneven cash flow (51%) and weak sales (48%)** ([Coleman Report summary of Fed SBCS](https://colemanreport.com/33-of-small-businesses-say-paying-debt-is-a-challenge-51-concerned-about-uneven-cash-flow/); [Fed SBCS 2025](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/reports/survey/2025/2025-report-on-employer-firms)). The 2026 edition again put rising costs first, with more than four in ten firms citing cost increases from tariffs ([Fed SBCS 2026](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/-/media/project/clevelandfedtenant/fsbsite/reports/2026/2026-report-on-employer-firms/2026-report-on-employer-firms.pdf)). An older Intuit study found that **32% of small firms had been unable to pay suppliers, loans, themselves or staff because of cash-flow problems** ([Intuit](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/282/quickbooks-study-cash-flow-woes-mean-a-third-of-small-businesses-cant-make-payroll-pay-bills)). The JPMorgan Chase Institute found the median small business holds only **27 days of cash**, and a quarter hold fewer than 13 ([JPMCI](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/institute/all-topics/business-growth-and-entrepreneurship/report-cash-flows-balances-and-buffer-days)).

Egypt's version is sharper. The S&P Global manufacturing index (PMI) fell to **47.2 in September 2026**; a reading below 50 means activity is shrinking. Firms raised their selling prices strongly ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/egypt/manufacturing-pmi/news/589380)) after input costs in April–May rose at their fastest pace since January 2023 ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/egypt/manufacturing-pmi/news/547684)). The World Bank's latest enterprise survey found **real sales down 11.4%, about 88% of investment paid from firms' own funds, and only about 3% financed by banks** ([EnterpriseAM](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2026/06/01/egyptian-firms-bypass-banks-to-fund-growth-from-their-own-pockets/)). When costs change every month and nobody lends, one cash or pricing mistake can sink a firm.

That evidence puts cash and pricing at the top of the list. The revenue forecast and the budget sit underneath as the base every other analysis needs. Marketing-mix models and churn models come last, because few small firms have enough data for them.

The core of the idea is showing how one decision ripples through the business. That is a driver model: price times volume gives revenue, revenue minus cost of goods gives margin, the days customers take to pay turn margin into cash timing, and cash timing sets the date the money runs out. Driver-based planning is also what separates good forecasters from poor ones. FP&A Trends reports that **77% of teams with fully driver-based models rate their accuracy as good, against 27% of teams with basic models** ([FP&A Trends](https://fpa-trends.com/article/2025-fpa-benchmarks-and-trends)).

The arithmetic is simple to explain. After a price cut, sales volume must rise by the size of the cut divided by (margin minus the cut) just to earn the same profit. So **a 5% cut at a 30% margin needs about 20% more volume** to break even ([MCP Analytics](https://mcpanalytics.ai/articles/price-elasticity-practical-guide-for-data-driven-decisions)). The same logic shows that a 10% price rise at a 30% margin still pays off unless volume falls by more than 25%. The chain of effects is pure arithmetic. The uncertainty lies in the assumptions, such as how customers react to a new price and how fast they pay, which is why every output must be a range.

The table below is the list this report recommends, ranked by how much each analysis helps a decision and how easy its data is to get. The ranking is inferred from the demand evidence above. The data and method columns draw on the practitioner and academic sources cited after the table.

| Rank | Forecast or what-if | Area | Exact minimum data | Method that holds up on SMB data | Decision it answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 13-week cash forecast plus 6–12-month runway | Finance | Reconciled bank balance at a fixed date; unpaid customer invoices by age, with due dates and each customer's real payment delay; unpaid supplier bills by age; payroll calendar with taxes; rent, loans, tax dates, renewals and equipment purchases; 6–12 months of bank transactions to learn the delays | A schedule of known payments in and out; simple time-series models only for "other receipts"; scenario switches | When cash falls below payroll, which invoices to chase, which payments to delay, whether to draw on credit |
| 2 | Price and cost pass-through what-if (including FX and tariffs) | Sales / finance | Price and unit cost by product; volumes; to measure how sales respond to price, sales at 3+ different price points with promotions marked | Break-even volume arithmetic; regression only where prices actually changed; time-boxed price tests | How much to reprice, which products to protect, what an FX move does to margin |
| 3 | Hire, new branch or lost-client scenario | Finance | A working driver-based P&L and cash model (rows 1 and 4) plus salary, rent or contract terms | Driver switches, sensitivity tables, simple Monte Carlo simulation | Can we afford one hire in March; what losing the biggest client does to the runway |
| 4 | Budget and P&L projection, updated monthly | Finance / accounting | 12–24 months of monthly P&L by account; headcount and salaries; known contracts; one-off items marked | Driver-based model, prior year plus growth, monthly re-forecast | Spending limits, targets, loan applications |
| 5 | Sales forecast by month, product, channel or customer | Sales | 24–36 months of monthly sales (or 2+ years weekly), so a model sees at least two seasonal cycles; consistent product codes; returns handled the same way throughout; promotions and price changes recorded | Seasonal naive ("same as last year"), exponential smoothing (ETS), Theta, regression with known drivers, and combinations of these | Targets, purchasing, staffing |
| 6 | Break-even and unit economics per product or branch | Finance | List of fixed costs; variable cost per unit or order; price; for customer-acquisition payback, marketing spend and new customers per month | Standard formulas with sensitivity tables | Go/no-go on products and branches; minimum prices |
| 7 | Demand and inventory reorder | Operations | Sales lines (date, product code, quantity, price, store), stock on hand, supplier lead times, minimum order quantities | Croston-type methods for slow, irregular items; ETS per product; reorder point = expected demand during the lead time + safety stock | Purchase orders; cash tied up in stock |
| 8 | Staffing from sales | Operations | 8+ weeks (ideally a year) of till (POS) sales by 15-minute slot or part of day; timesheets | Weekly seasonal averages or ETS, converted to labour hours at a target labour cost % | Staff rosters, hiring |
| 9 | Marketing acquisition cost and return on ad spend; full marketing-mix model only for the few with enough data | Marketing | Simple version: monthly spend by channel, new customers, revenue. Marketing-mix model: 2–3 years of weekly spend by channel, revenue, promotions and holidays | Trend and payback arithmetic; holdout or regional tests; Bayesian marketing-mix model only with enough history | Moving budget between channels |
| 10 | Churn and customer lifetime value | Marketing / sales | 12+ months of order history with a stable customer ID (one row per order); for subscriptions, start and cancel dates | Cohort retention tables; BG/NBD and Gamma-Gamma models; survival curves | Retention spending; maximum acceptable cost per new customer |

Three cautions sit behind the data column. First, minimum history is a rule of thumb, not a law. Hyndman and Kostenko conclude that **"there are no simple rules about sample size."** What matters is how many parameters a model estimates and how noisy the data is ([Hyndman & Kostenko, Foresight](https://ideas.repec.org/a/for/ijafaa/y2007i6p12-15.html)).

Second, the data-hungry rows really do need a lot of data. Google's own documentation for its Meridian marketing-mix tool asks for **at least two years of weekly data for regional models and three years for national models, or three years if only monthly data exist** ([Google Meridian](https://developers.google.com/meridian/docs/pre-modeling/collect-data)). Guides on measuring how sales respond to price want 3–6 months of data that include several price changes of about 10–15% ([Symson](https://www.symson.com/blog/what-kind-of-data-quality-do-you-need-to-run-price-elasticity-analysis-for-your-products)). Lifetime-value models need one row per order with a stable customer ID ([MetricGate](https://metricgate.com/docs/clv-bg-nbd/)).

Third, the 13-week cash forecast needs the least history of all. It only needs current balances plus the timing of customer payments, supplier bills and payroll, rolled forward each week ([Centime](https://www.centime.com/articles/13-week-cash-flow-forecast); [Float](https://www.floatapp.com/blog/13-week-cash-flow-forecast)). That is exactly why a newcomer should lead with it.

Egypt changes what data a client actually holds. The Tax Authority's e-invoicing platform had processed **more than 1.5 billion documents by May 2025** ([Shared Services Link](https://sharedserviceslink.com/news/egypt-s-e-invoicing-system-surpasses-1-5-billion-documents-)). The duty to issue e-receipts to consumers was extended to new groups of taxpayers during 2025 ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2025/07/tnf-egypt-new-taxpayers-required-to-comply-with-mandate-to-issue-electronic-receipts-for-b2c-transactions.html)). So formal firms often have two to four years of structured sales history that runs through the March 2024 float.

Cost data is a different story. The simplified tax regime under Law 6 of 2025, for firms with turnover up to EGP 20 million, explicitly does not require complex accounting records ([EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/egypt-introduces-tax-incentives-and-benefits-for-small-enterprises)). Historically, only **8–14% of Egyptian household businesses kept regular books**, though that data is from 1998–2006 ([ERF data portal](https://erfdataportal.com/index.php/catalog/27/variable/V16431)). In practice, Egyptian clients will often have rich sales data but thin cost data, so the reports will need cost assumptions supplied by the owner and clearly labelled.

## Spreadsheets still run planning, and most of the work is gathering data

Even well-staffed finance teams plan in spreadsheets. The Association for Financial Professionals (AFP) found in its 2025 survey that **96% of financial planning and analysis (FP&A) staff use spreadsheets for planning**. It concluded that dedicated planning software has not fixed the problem of unreliable data ([AFP](https://www.financialprofessionals.org/about/learn-more/press-releases/Details/survey-lack-of-reliable-and-accessible-data-holds-fp-a-back-from-success-with-technology)).

FP&A Trends' 2025 survey, as reported in published summaries, found that finance teams spend **about 46% of their time collecting and checking data**. About 55% of organisations need more than five working days to produce a forecast, and **only 18% can run a what-if scenario in under a day**; 39% blame a lack of flexible tools ([FP&A Trends](https://fpa-trends.com/article/2025-fpa-benchmarks-and-trends)). Accuracy is mediocre even so: 52% of finance leaders told Vena that their revenue forecasts miss the actual result by more than 6% ([Vena](https://www.venasolutions.com/blog/fpa-trends-predictions)).

These are firms with full finance departments. So the pain a forecasting service removes is not a shortage of algorithms. It is the slow, error-prone work of getting clean data into a model and re-running it every time the owner asks "what if?"

Below about 50 employees, planning is thinner still: an annual Excel budget built by the owner or an outside accountant, a glance at the bank balance, and gut feel for pricing and hiring. No recent survey measures this directly, so it is an inference. But an older RBS/NatWest survey found that **41% of UK SMEs had never run a cash-flow forecast** ([Global Banking & Finance Review](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/poor-cash-flow-management-stunting-growth-of-uk-smes/)).

The forecasts built into accounting software only project forward from invoice due dates and bank feeds; Xero's covers 30 to 180 days depending on the plan. They cannot answer questions like "what if I hire?" or "what if my biggest client leaves?" ([Finoya](https://www.finoya.ai/blog/ledger-ai-cash-flow-limits/)). The Excel tool a business-analysis graduate usually reaches for is FORECAST.ETS. It automates exponential smoothing and detects seasonality. But it fails with a #NUM! error if dates are not evenly spaced, and it quietly fills up to 30% of missing points by averaging their neighbours ([Microsoft](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/excel/forecast-ets-function)).

The same data problems come up again and again. Books are not reconciled. Owner withdrawals are mixed with business spending. Bank lines are not categorised. Product codes change. Nobody recorded promotions or price changes. Cleaning all this up is most of the work.

Egypt and the wider region add manual bookkeeping and payment problems. Research on Arab SMEs in Egypt, Jordan and Iraq finds that many still keep accounts by hand ([Acopen](https://acopen.umsida.ac.id/index.php/acopen/article/view/11215)). A study of Egyptian firms found basic management-accounting tools far more common than advanced ones ([EKB journal](https://atasu.journals.ekb.eg/article_37839.html)). In the UAE, 96% of SMEs accept digital payments but only 39% use a connected business-management tool, and **30% struggle to keep track of unpaid invoices** ([Zawya / Visa](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/research-studies/visa-report-finds-96-of-uae-smes-surveyed-use-digital-payments-while-financial-administration-slows-business-operations-1652947)). A Visa and MSMEDA study of Egyptian SMEs found that 53% had started taking digital payments in the past two years, and that SMEs want **"access to data-driven insights"** from their payment providers ([Visa](https://www.visa.com/en-eg/newsroom/egypt-smes-digital-payments-growth-study)). That is the most direct evidence of local demand for analytics this research found.

An early honesty test is worth noting. The often-repeated claim that "82% of small businesses fail because of cash-flow problems" traces back to a study by a U.S. Bank executive from roughly two decades ago, and nobody can find the original ([Forwardly](https://www.forwardai.com/knowledge-center/?p=785)). A forecasting service that sells credibility should not open its pitch with a number it cannot verify. Intuit's 32% figure and JPMCI's 27-day cash cushion make the same point with real sources.

The founder's training fits better than it might seem. Typical business-forecasting courses, such as Macquarie University's Excel forecasting series on Coursera, teach naive methods, moving averages, Holt and Winters smoothing, decomposition and regression ([Coursera](https://www-cloudfront-alias.coursera.org/learn/excel-business-forecasting-time-series)). Those are the right tools for short data series. In the M4 forecasting competition, which covered 100,000 series, **12 of the 17 most accurate methods were combinations of mostly statistical methods, and the pure machine-learning entries did poorly** ([International Institute of Forecasters](https://forecasters.org/blog/2018/06/19/the-m-competitions-and-their-far-reaching-contributions-to-the-theory-and-practice-of-forecasting)).

What these courses under-teach is the practical finance work: the 13-week cash method, the timing gap between profit on paper and cash in the bank, how the three financial statements link together, and driver-based budgeting. Accountants feel the same gap from their side. Xero found that about 85% of US accounting practices now offer advisory services, and 35% name clients' "evolving expectations for forward-looking strategic advice" as a top challenge ([Xero](https://www.xero.com/us/media-releases/us-state-of-the-industry-report-2025/)). In Intuit's survey, 79% of accountants expect advisory work to grow ([Intuit](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/small-business-data/accountant-tech-survey-2025/)). That pressure makes accountants the natural partners for this service.

Three trends matter more than any single row. First, between the $50–500-a-month do-it-yourself apps and the $3,000-plus human CFOs there is a gap, and a fixed-scope recurring report can sit in it.

Second, the venture-funded tools that once served small companies have gone. Lucanet absorbed Causal in October 2024 ([BARC](https://barc.com/causal-acquisition-by-lucanet/)). Finmark shut down on 1 April 2026 after BILL bought it ([CheckThat.ai](https://checkthat.ai/brands/finmark/alternatives)). HiBob folded Mosaic into its HR suite ([Burncast](https://blog.burncast.io/posts/startup-financial-modeling-gap/)). Meanwhile investors are backing the top of the market: Pigment raised **$250M at a $2.3B valuation in October 2026** ([WOWTALE](https://en.wowtale.net/2026/10/22/235679/)). A founder with no capital should take this as a warning against building general-purpose planning software.

Third, "upload a spreadsheet and ask for a forecast" is now nearly free, but it is not reliable. On the Vals.ai Excel modelling benchmark, the best model passes only **64% of numerical checks** ([Vals.ai](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/emb)). The WorkstreamBench study of finance spreadsheet tasks found that AI agents typed in fixed values where formulas belonged and left harder tasks unfinished ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22664v1)). Owners can produce forecasts themselves. What they cannot easily get is a forecast they can trust.

The closest regional competitor is **Kudwa. It raised $1.1M in March 2026 and targets firms with 50–500 employees and turnover up to $80M** in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, the US and India ([Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/en/2026/03/kudwa-lands-1-1-million-funding-scale-ai-finance-manager-platform)). Its AI forecasts are available inside the Wafeq accounting app "entirely in Arabic" ([Wafeq](https://www.wafeq.com/en-eg/press-releases/media-center/wafeq's-partnership-with-kudwa)). But it is software for larger Gulf firms, not a done-for-you service for small Egyptian ones.

Foodics BI added AI forecasting for restaurants in August 2025 ([Absolute Geeks](https://www.absolutegeeks.com/tech-news/foodics-bi-launches-ai-analytics-tool-helps-restaurants-boost-profits-by-20/)), so restaurant demand forecasting is a poor place to start. Daftra, the Egyptian-founded accounting suite, showed no AI forecasting. Odoo partners, who sell EGP 125,000-plus implementations, have every reason to resell an analytics service on top. Both look more like distribution partners than rivals. Arabic-language searches found only offerings for large companies, such as SAP's ([SAP MENA](https://www.sap.com/mena-ar/resources/how-ai-redefines-sales-forecasting)). Wafeq and Pigment both now let Claude and ChatGPT read their data through MCP connectors ([Wafeq AI](https://www.wafeq.com/en/wafeq-ai)), which makes it cheaper to build on top of existing systems than to rebuild them.

So the gap is a package nobody sells: a forecast, plus local economic scenarios, plus a decision memo, delivered every month as a human-checked report, in Arabic, priced in EGP or at modest Gulf rates, for firms with fewer than about 100 employees.

The crowded areas to avoid are general-purpose planning software, Shopify inventory apps, Xero/QuickBooks cash-flow tools in the US and UK, restaurant analytics in MENA, and generic "ask your data" chatbots. The threats to watch are Kudwa or Wafeq moving to smaller firms or into Egypt, Daftra adding built-in forecasting, and Copilot and Claude for Excel becoming reliable enough that accountants do this work themselves.

## Code computes the numbers, Claude explains them, and nobody can predict the pound

### Statistical models plus one foundation model, chosen by testing on past data

Pretrained "foundation" forecasting models now top the big public rankings. In the GIFT-Eval comparison published with the Chronos-2 paper, **Chronos-2 wins 79.8% of head-to-head comparisons on probabilistic accuracy**, ahead of TiRex and TimesFM-2.5 ([Chronos-2 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.15821)). It also leads fev-bench, a set of 100 forecasting tasks ([fev-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26468v2)).

Independent tests tell a different story for short monthly or quarterly data. A NeurIPS 2025 paper found that **statistical models and their combinations beat existing foundation models by more than 8.2% on one error measure (sCRPS) and more than 20% on another (MASE)**, and argued that statistical models' accuracy is routinely under-reported ([NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/130449)). On monthly data from the M3 competition and a tourism dataset, the classic Theta and ETS methods beat a small Moirai foundation model ([VisionTS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.17253)).

The safest design runs both kinds side by side. Nixtla's free statsforecast library fits several standard models (AutoETS, AutoTheta, AutoARIMA, AutoCES) and picks the best one for each series by testing on past data ([Nixtla](https://www.nixtla.io/blog/statsforecast-automatic-model-selection)). Chronos runs alongside them with no training needed, together with a "same as last year" baseline. A backtest then decides between them by re-running each model on the client's own history to see how it would have done.

Machine-learning models such as LightGBM, which won the Walmart M5 competition ([M5 paper](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/M5_accuracy_competition.pdf)), are only worth using for clients with many products or branches.

Licences decide which models can be used in a paid service. Chronos uses the Apache-2.0 licence ([Chronos repo](https://github.com/amazon-science/chronos-forecasting)) and so does TimesFM-2.5, both of which allow commercial use. But **TimesFM-3's default model files and Moirai-2.0 are licensed for non-commercial use only** ([beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/timesfm-vs-chronos-vs-moirai-time-series-foundation-model-licence-bigquery-sagemaker-cost-2026); [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/Salesforce/moirai-2.0-R-small)).

No graphics card (GPU) is needed for SMB-sized work. One test measured Chronos-Bolt Tiny at about 0.06 seconds per forecast on a desktop processor. Chronos-2 took about 1.79 seconds with a history of 1,032 data points, far longer than any SMB series ([CITRAS-FM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.10798)). Nixtla's hosted TimeGPT service gives 50 free requests a month, and 10,000 for $99, if a hosted option is ever wanted ([Nixtla docs](https://docs.nixtla.io/docs/getting-started-timegpt_subscription_plans_and_pricing)).

### The language model organises data, writes code and explains, but never makes up a figure

Peer-reviewed research is blunt about LLMs as forecasters. At NeurIPS 2024, Tan and colleagues removed the language model from three popular LLM-based forecasters, or replaced it with a much simpler component, and **accuracy did not fall and often improved** ([NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6ed5bf446f59e2c6646d23058c86424b-Abstract.html)). Another study found LLMs less accurate than ARIMA, a standard statistical model ([Cao & Wang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.04867)). When LLMs write data-analysis code, the most common crash comes from referring to columns that do not exist ([arXiv 2212.09248](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.09248)). Running the code catches that error. Code that runs but uses the wrong logic slips through.

So the division of labour should be strict. **Claude** maps messy Arabic or English column headings onto a fixed format, using Anthropic's structured outputs feature, which forces replies into a set JSON format ([Anthropic models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview)); it also suggests cleaning steps, writes the Python (pandas) code, and turns the calculated tables into plain-language explanations. **Code** produces every number. **A checking script** then confirms that each figure in the written explanation appears in, or follows from, the calculated results, and flags anything else for a human to review.

Claude's hosted code-running sandbox includes pandas and statsmodels, but it runs on one processor with no internet access ([Anthropic code execution](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/code-execution-tool)). So the forecasting engine belongs in the founder's own Python project, which Claude can write and debug with the founder.

The AI cost is tiny. Anthropic's current list prices per million tokens (input/output) are **$2/$10 for Sonnet 5.5, $0.10/$0.50 for Haiku 5.5 and $4/$20 for Opus 5.5** ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). One explanation pass on Sonnet, reading 30,000 tokens and writing 5,000, costs about 11 cents. Even a report built with a dozen Opus passes for cleaning, scenarios, writing and checking costs only a few dollars.

### Outside economic data helps most as scenarios, not as model inputs

Plenty of economic data is free:

| Source | What it gives | Access |
|---|---|---|
| FRED (US Federal Reserve data) | Economic series | Free key ([FreeAPIHub](https://freeapihub.com/blog/fred-api-tutorial-python-economic-data)) |
| World Bank Indicators API | Country indicators | No key needed ([World Bank](https://datahelpdesk.worldbank.org/knowledgebase/articles/889392-about-the-indicators-api)) |
| IMF data services | Country statistics | Free account required ([IMF](https://data.imf.org/en/Resource-Pages/IMF-API)) |
| Python `holidays` package | Public holidays | About 250 countries ([holidays](https://simple-repository.app.cern.ch/project/holidays)) |
| Open-Meteo | Weather | Free only for non-commercial use ([Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/)) |
| Google Trends | Search interest | Official access only through an application-based test programme; the popular unofficial pytrends library was retired in April 2025 ([Konabayev](https://konabayev.com/blog/google-trends-api-landscape-2026/)) |

This research found no confirmed data feed from the Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) or CAPMAS, Egypt's statistics agency, so local data will be downloaded by hand at first.

The evidence that outside data improves forecasts is positive but modest. One retail study found that adding every available outside variable to a DeepAR model cut forecast error (MASE) by **6.5%, and that the choice of model mattered more** ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374286376_Robust_Sales_forecasting_Using_Deep_Learning_with_Static_and_Dynamic_Covariates)). A study of automotive SMEs found that models using 30 economic and industry indicators clearly beat models that used sales history alone ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41270-025-00436-0)). Drivers known in advance, such as holidays, planned promotions and planned price changes, help most. Slow monthly economic series rarely help a model with only 36 data points, because there are too few observations to measure their effect.

Egypt makes outside context both more valuable and harder to use. The pound went from **about 30.9 to about 50 per dollar on 6 March 2024** ([FrontierView](https://frontierview.com/?p=55205)). The central bank's main interest rate peaked at 27.25% before being cut to 19% in February 2026 ([Central Banking](https://www.centralbanking.com/central-banks/monetary-policy/monetary-policy-decisions/7975038/egypt-cuts-rates-by-100bp-and-lowers-reserve-requirements)). Urban inflation was **13.9% in September 2026**, against a 7% target the CBE now expects to reach only in the second half of 2027 ([Daily News Egypt](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/2026/10/10/egypts-annual-urban-inflation-eases-to-13-9-in-september/)), and fuel prices jumped 14–17% in March 2026 ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/egypt/inflation-cpi/news/590967)). A model trained on that history learns the shocks as if they were normal.

The workable approach is to forecast in inflation-adjusted or dollar terms, then convert back to Egyptian pounds under explicit assumptions, such as the pound at 45, 50 and 55 to the dollar. Markers for Ramadan and Eid belong in every retail and food model, because those holidays move about 11 days earlier each year and a fixed-month seasonal pattern misplaces them. Industry news and competitor moves should appear as sourced background, never as numbers fed into the model.

### What-if answers come from driver models; cause-and-effect claims mostly fail on SMB data

A driver model with Monte Carlo simulation tries thousands of combinations within each assumption's range. It produces a low, middle and high outcome (P10/P50/P90) and three named scenarios. It needs assumptions, not big data.

Measuring cause and effect statistically, such as how many sales a marketing channel caused, mostly does not work with small amounts of data. Google's Meridian and Meta's Robyn marketing-mix tools are free to use, but running Meridian in-house is estimated to cost about $76K–129K in the first year in staff time ([mbuzz](https://mbuzz.co/articles/best-mmm-software)). Google's own Meridian example, two years of weekly data across 12 channels, leaves only **about four data points for each effect being estimated**, which Google itself calls a low-sample case ([Google Meridian](https://developers.google.com/meridian/docs/pre-modeling/collect-data)). Practitioners say a small budget, one dominant channel or less than a year of history makes marketing-mix results unreliable ([The Growth Folks](https://thegrowthfolks.com/blog/marketing-mix-modelling-small-budgets/)). More advanced statistical methods for cause and effect (double machine learning) only come with guarantees for large samples ([Chernozhukov et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.00060)). For marketing questions, the honest product is acquisition-cost and payback arithmetic plus a planned test, such as pausing ads in one area and comparing.

### Data connections: start with file uploads

Every accounting and online-shop connection has a catch. **Xero** moved app developers to paid tiers in March 2026, and its free tier allows only five connected businesses ([Truto](https://truto.one/blog/xero-api-pricing-changes-2026-costs-tiers-and-how-to-minimize-egress/)). **QuickBooks** charges for heavy use of some data requests under its App Partner Program ([Apideck](https://www.apideck.com/blog/quickbooks-api-pricing-and-the-intuit-app-partner-program)). **Shopify** apps can see only the last 60 days of orders without special approval ([Shopify](https://shopify.dev/changelog/apps-now-need-shopify-approval-to-read-orders-older-than-60-days)). **Odoo**'s data connection requires a Custom plan on its online version ([Odoo](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/developer/reference/external_rpc_api.html)), and **Foodics** data access must be switched on by the account owner, on an Advanced plan or with a purchased licence ([Foodics](https://help.foodics.com/hc/en-us/articles/7494382541084-Foodics-API-Adapter)). Excel and CSV uploads, by contrast, cover almost every early client with no approvals needed, so they should be the only data connection at launch.

### Accuracy limits are built in, so the product must show its own error

Financial and market outcomes are close to impossible to forecast. In the M6 competition, **only 23.3% of 163 teams beat a simple benchmark's forecast accuracy, and about 60% lost money** on their investment portfolios ([M6 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.13357v1)). Sudden breaks like the 2024 float, new products with no history, promotions and stock shortages nobody recorded, and data covering less than two full years are not fixed by a better model either.

No published accuracy benchmark exists for SMB monthly sales in high-inflation economies, so accuracy has to be measured for each client and shown. Each report should show how the chosen model would have performed on past data compared with "same as last year," and give forecast ranges together with a check on past data that those ranges really contained the actual result as often as they claim to; statsforecast can produce such ranges for any model ([Nixtla](https://nixtlaverse.nixtla.io/statsforecast/docs/tutorials/conformalprediction.html)). A fan chart with base, downside and upside scenarios replaces any single "AI prediction."

The minimum set of tools is small enough for a beginner to build with Claude in roughly four to eight weeks of focused work, and to run for about $20–100 a month with pilot clients. Both figures are estimates, not measured results. The open-source Darts library can also wrap both standard models and Chronos-2 behind one interface if that proves simpler ([Darts](https://unit8co.github.io/darts/generated_api/darts.models.forecasting.chronos2_model.html)).

| Layer | Tool | Job | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data intake | Excel/CSV upload; Claude Haiku or Sonnet with structured outputs | Map the client's columns onto a fixed format; the owner confirms the mapping | Claude suggests, pandas code applies |
| Storage | SQLite, later Postgres | Original files, cleaned tables, economic data with source and date | Every outside number records where and when it came from |
| Forecast | statsforecast plus Chronos-Bolt or Chronos-2 on an ordinary processor | Run several models, test them on past data, produce ranges | Beat "same as last year", or say so |
| Context | World Bank, IMF, FRED, CBE downloads, `holidays` | Exchange rates, inflation, interest rates, Ramadan and Eid markers | Feed into the model only if past-data testing shows it helps; otherwise use as a scenario |
| What-if | Python driver model plus Monte Carlo simulation | Low/middle/high outcomes and three named scenarios | Every assumption listed and approved by the client |
| Explanation | Claude Sonnet | Plain-language memo quoting the calculated figures | A checking script blocks any number it cannot trace |
| Output | HTML/PDF report, later a simple web portal (e.g. Streamlit) | Fan chart, past-accuracy table, assumptions, limits | A human reviews before sending |

## Sell a reviewed report first, priced between Fiverr and a part-time CFO

### Starting with a service is the proven path; Bench shows the trap

Software companies that grew out of service businesses tend to follow the same sequence. **37signals** was a web-design firm that built the Basecamp project tool for its own client work and launched it on 5 February 2004 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/37signals)); within about 18 months Basecamp earned more than the design work ([Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/how-basecamp-grew-side-project-one-world-s-most-thriving-startups)). **Craig Hewitt** ran a fixed-package podcast-editing service that led to Castos, his software product, now about 80% of seven-figure annual recurring revenue ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/starting-with-a-productized-service-and-building-a-7-figure-product-around-it-DGvnTx1VofDBKj0kWaDG)). **An analytics founder** whose software had stalled at 12 users and $340 a month switched to a $1,500 flat-fee audit, automated it, and turned the service clients into the product's first 40 subscribers, by his own unverified account ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/insightlab/the-productized-service-pivot-5ff4)).

The warning is Bench Accounting, a venture-funded, tech-driven bookkeeping firm with nearly 500 staff that shut down suddenly on 27 December 2024 ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/vancouver-fintech-company-bench-accounting-announces-sudden-shutdown/)). Commentators blame too much automation with too little human review, producing books that "look clean but contain systematic errors," and prices too low to sustain ([Build Mode](https://buildmodehq.substack.com/p/bench-accounting-why-tech-enabled)).

Starting with a service suits a beginner with no capital: it brings in cash from the first client and shows which outputs people actually pay for. That holds only if human review is never the part that gets cut.

### Market size: millions of firms, but a small core that can pay

According to the 2017/18 census, **99.7% of Egypt's roughly 3.7 million businesses have fewer than 50 employees, 91.7% are micro-businesses, and about 53% are informal** ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/sme-and-entrepreneurship-policy-in-egypt_195d9c43-en/full-report/sme-and-entrepreneurship-performance-and-characteristics-in-egypt_a963c2a5.html)). The realistic buyers are formal small and medium firms with digital records, probably tens of thousands rather than millions. The Federation of Egyptian Industries alone represents about 104,000 industrial businesses ([Union of Arab Chambers](https://uac-org.org/en/arabchamber/affiliated-members/federation-of-egyptian-industries)). Saudi Arabia has about 1.7 million active business registrations ([Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/gcc/monshaat-report-saudi-commercial-registrations-hit-17mln-hxxoyd1b)), and the UAE had 557,000 SMEs at the end of 2022 ([WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/hszrgtp9-557000-smes-operated-uae-end-2022-minister)). Gulf firms are more formal and richer per firm.

The suggested prices are inferred from the reference points, converted at about EGP 48 per dollar, the level of 2026 bank quotes ([Alyaum](https://www.alyaum.com/ampArticle/6540849)). No study exists of what Egyptian SMEs are willing to pay, so treat these as guesses to test.

The arithmetic shows why the Gulf matters. Ten Egyptian monthly clients at EGP 4,000 bring in about EGP 40,000 (roughly $830) a month. Adding just three Gulf monthly clients at $500 brings the total to roughly $2,330, nearly three times as much. Charge for the result and the delivery time, never by the hour, because AI shrinks the hours.

Sell three fixed-scope products. The entry product is **a one-off "cash and pricing health check"** delivered in about five working days, containing a 13-week cash forecast with the date cash runs short and a ranked list of options (collect, delay, cut, borrow), a table showing how cost changes should feed into prices, and exchange-rate and inflation scenarios. The recurring product is **a monthly update**: a new forecast, a comparison with last month, and a short call. The third is **a bank-ready financial projection** co-signed by a partner accountant.

The bank projection has an advantage built into the system. Since 2021 the CBE has required banks to lend 25% of their loan books to micro, small and medium businesses ([Enterprise](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2021/02/23/msmes-will-have-an-easier-time-getting-bank-finance-thanks-to-the-cbe/)). MSMEDA, Egypt's small-business development agency, paid out about EGP 6bn in 2024 ([Business Today Egypt](https://www.businesstodayegypt.com/amp/1/5837/MSMEDA-invests-EGP-6B-into-MSME-sector-in-2024-plans)). Yet only about 3% of business investment is financed by banks, so a credible cash projection is often what a loan application is missing.

### Finding clients with no money: borrow trust from accountants and software installers

Small firms already ask their accountant, bookkeeper or lawyer when they need financial help ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/software/learn/fractional-cfo)). That makes two or three Egyptian accounting offices the strongest channel. They hold client books and e-invoice data, their clients are pushing them for forward-looking advice, and they can resell the reports under their own name or refer clients in exchange for a share of revenue. Some will see the founder as a competitor, since accountants are starting to offer CFO services themselves ([Relay](https://www.relayfi.com/blog/fractional-cfo-services-for-accountants)).

The second channel is Odoo partners and Daftra resellers, who spent 2022–2026 selling e-invoicing compliance and can add an analytics service on top. Freelance marketplaces (Khamsat, Mostaql, Upwork, Fiverr) bring quick first reviews but heavy price competition.

Egypt has about **60 million Facebook users and about 18 million on LinkedIn** ([NapoleonCat](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/social-media-users-in-egypt/2026/)). Arabic Facebook groups for merchants and online sellers reach far more owners than LinkedIn. LinkedIn suits exporters, medium-sized firms and Gulf buyers.

The Gulf also has good timing. Saudi e-invoicing integration reaches every taxpayer with revenue above **SAR 187,500 by 1 February 2027** ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/07/saudi-arabia-e-invoicing-mandatory-for-taxpayers-with-revenue-above-sar-187500.html)), and UAE e-invoicing becomes mandatory in January and July 2027 ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/07/tnf-uae-phased-implementation-of-e-invoicing-beginning-january-1-2027.html)). That mirrors what Egypt went through in 2022–25, so an Egyptian founder who has learned to turn e-invoice data into decisions arrives with a transferable skill, and Egypt is already an established Arabic-language back office for Gulf companies ([MEA Tech Watch](https://meatechwatch.com/2026/04/24/itida-ceo-inaugurates-alshaya-groups-first-offshoring-global-talent-center-in-cairo-marking-20-years-in-egypt/)).

Cold outreach is a numbers game with known odds. About 26–30% of LinkedIn connection requests are accepted, and about 7–10% of new connections reply to a message ([Belkins](https://belkins.io/resources/b2b-cold-outreach-benchmarks-2025-report); [Expandi](https://expandi.io/?p=14040)). Cold emails get replies about 1.5–3.5% of the time ([Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/cold-email-outbound-statistics)). These figures come from US and European data, but they suggest that 300 targeted LinkedIn requests lead to roughly 6–9 conversations and one to three clients. Getting ten clients from cold outreach alone would take about 1,000–1,500 contacts, or three to six months. Referrals and marketplaces shorten that. Focusing on one narrow group with a named problem raises reply rates and makes each case study reusable. Examples: the falling pound for importers, unpaid invoices for distributors, Ramadan seasonality for retailers.

Trust should be built in cheap steps. Start with a sample report based on made-up data, then a small paid offer rather than a long free audit ([Heyworkshop](https://heyworkshop.substack.com/p/stop-pitching-the-whole-project-offer)), and a few discounted pilots in exchange for a case study that names the client ([Bright Coding](https://blog.brightcoding.dev/2026/09/01/how-to-get-your-first-10-clients-as-a-freelancer)). For each prospect, a test showing how the method would have performed on their own past year is the most persuasive proof available. A low-cost certificate such as CFI's Financial Modeling & Valuation Analyst (FMVA), at roughly $297–847 a year ([Vena](https://www.venasolutions.com/blog/fpa-certifications-courses)), can come later.

Egypt's accounting profession is still governed by Law 133 of 1951, with a government register of accountants ([World Bank ROSC](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/589181468023039456/pdf/350800EGT0rosc1aa.pdf)), so a registered accountant partner provides the signature a beginner cannot. The founder should present themselves as "a business analyst using AI-assisted forecasting tools," not a financial adviser or CFO. That is both more honest and legally safer.

### Costs, tax and getting paid

AI costs are negligible; tax, registration and collecting money are not.

**Tax.** Egypt's Law 6 of 2025 lets businesses with turnover up to EGP 20 million pay a tax on turnover instead of profit. The rate starts at **0.4% (below EGP 500,000) and rises to 1.5% (EGP 10–20 million)** ([Taxspoc](https://www.taxspoc.com/articles/egypt-transforming-taxation-in-2025-key-highlights-of-laws-no-5-6-and-7); [Riad & Riad](https://riad-riad.com/new-tax-incentives-for-smes-understanding-egypts-law-no-6-of-2025/)), and freelancers can apply ([Enterprise](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2026/10/11/your-guide-to-registering-as-a-freelancer-in-egypt/)). But **consultants who earn 90% or more of their revenue from one or two clients are excluded** ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/tax-authorities/1791346/new-tax-incentives-in-law-no-6-of-2025-for-small-businesses)), which hurts exactly when a newcomer depends on one main client.

**E-invoicing and VAT.** Self-employed people must register for e-invoicing if they work with companies that issue e-invoices ([Enterprise](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2023/04/18/tax-authority-exempts-some-self-employed-professionals-from-e-invoicing/)). VAT on consulting sold abroad was not researched; ask an accountant.

**Getting paid.** Stripe is not available to Egyptian businesses ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/i-m-from-egypt-and-i-cant-add-a-payment-method/169088)). Foreign clients can pay through Payoneer ([Payoneer](https://www.payoneer.com/th/success-story/mohamed-eldeeb/)) or by Wise transfer into an Egyptian bank account, although Egyptian residents reportedly cannot open a Wise account themselves ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/?p=147180)). Local clients can pay through Paymob, bank transfer or InstaPay. Check fees and eligibility directly with each provider before sending the first foreign invoice.

### Legal and liability: stay out of investment advice and protect client data

No source showed that a licence is needed in Egypt to prepare forecasts, budgets and scenario analysis about a company's own business, though a lawyer should confirm this. The Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) regulates advice on buying and selling securities, including AI-driven investment advice, which it allows only for licensed portfolio managers ([Business Today Egypt](https://businesstodayegypt.com/amp/1/4636/FRA-releases-regulatory-framework-for-use-of-AI-powered-financial)). It also warns that people who promote unlicensed investments online can be held liable ([Amwal Al Ghad](https://en.amwalalghad.com/?p=200547)). The founder should never recommend stocks or other securities, never handle client money, and never call the work "investment advice."

Liability is managed by contract. Consulting and accounting engagement letters typically cap liability at the fees paid, exclude indirect losses and lost profits ([2024 engagement letter](https://dms.my-cpe.com/engagement-letter/2024-TAX-CONSULTING-ENGAGEMENT-LETTER.pdf); [Daeryun Law](https://www.daeryunlaw.com/us/insights/business-consulting-in-nyc)), and say only the client may rely on the work ([Irish Tax Institute](https://taxinstitute.ie/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Guidelines-on-provisions-to-include-in-a-ltr-of-engagement-updated-Dec-2017.doc.pdf)). The founder's engagement letter should also state that the outputs are projections rather than guarantees, that results depend on the data the client supplies, that the work is not legal, tax, audit or investment advice, that AI tools are used and a human reviews the output, and which country's law applies. An Egyptian lawyer should check that these terms hold up. Do not guarantee accuracy; offer a free revision instead.

Data protection becomes urgent within days. The rules implementing Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law were issued on 1 November 2025 with a one-year grace period, so **enforcement is expected from about 1 November 2026** ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/are/legal-updates/egypt-s-pdpl-executive-regulations-issued-one-year-compliance-countdown-begins)). Sending personal data abroad generally requires the other country to have equal protection, or the person's consent, or regulator approval. Organisations handling up to 100,000 records are reportedly exempt from licence fees ([Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/egypt-important-data-protection-update/)). Reported fines reach EGP 5 million for operating without a licence ([Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/guides/legal-landscapes/egypt-data-protection-cybersecurity/)).

Forecasting mostly needs totals, not personal details. Asking clients to remove or code customer and employee names keeps the risk low. But sending any personal data to a US AI provider still counts as sending it abroad. Only paid AI services should ever see client data. Anthropic contractually does not train its models on data sent through its paid business API ([AI Business](https://aibusiness.com/nlp/anthropic-will-not-train-its-ai-on-clients-data)), though sources disagree on whether it keeps logs for 7 or 30 days ([Anarlog](https://anarlog.so/blog/anthropic-data-retention-policy)); Google's free Gemini tier, by contrast, uses data to improve its products ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Oct/17/gemini-terms-of-service)). The AI provider should be named as a sub-processor in every engagement letter.

Professional liability insurance is probably impractical at launch. A clear scope, assumptions approved by the client and capped liability are the realistic substitutes.

## Verdict: buildable and sellable now as a reviewed service, a platform only once it repeats

The answer is **yes to building it, a qualified yes to selling it, and not yet to a platform**.

Building is the easy part. Every component is free or nearly free, runs on a laptop or a small server, and is within what a beginner can put together with Claude in weeks.

Selling is plausible for three reasons. The pain is real and acute in Egypt. Accountants are being pushed toward advisory work they lack the tools for. And nobody serves Egyptian firms with fewer than about 100 employees with Arabic, locally informed, human-checked decision reports. It is unproven because nobody has measured what those firms will pay, and because a newcomer starts with no reputation.

Building a platform first would put a beginner with no capital up against $20-a-month apps, venture-funded planning software, and AI features bundled free into QuickBooks and Xero. The evidence says that is a losing fight.

The best starting market, where good data meets real pain, is formal Egyptian firms with roughly 10–100 employees that already issue e-invoices: **distributors and wholesalers**, whose pain is unpaid invoices and purchasing tied to the exchange rate; **small manufacturers that import inputs**, who must reprice under cost shocks; and **online sellers not using Shopify**, whom the crowded Shopify forecasting apps do not reach. Saudi and UAE SMEs, served remotely, are the higher-paying second market. Restaurants have good data, but Foodics already sells them forecasting, so offer them menu-pricing what-ifs rather than demand forecasts.

The strongest objection is that owners can simply ask ChatGPT or Claude. That is true for owners who can clean their own data and judge the result, but they are not the customer. The customer is the owner who needs someone accountable to do it, explain it and update it next month.

Some gaps cannot be closed by any model or any amount of effort, and the report sold to clients has to say so plainly.

| Gap that cannot be closed | Why | How to handle it honestly in the client report |
|---|---|---|
| Predicting exchange rates, inflation, competitor moves or sudden demand shocks | Market outcomes are close to random; only 23.3% of M6 teams beat a simple benchmark | Present economic conditions as named scenarios the owner chooses (pound at 45/50/55), labelled "not a prediction", with sourced, dated figures |
| Reliable forecasts across sudden breaks and short histories | The 2024 float, and histories shorter than two years, defeat every model | Model only the period after the break, or work in inflation-adjusted or dollar terms; widen the ranges; show how the model did on past data compared with "same as last year" |
| Marketing return and which channel caused which sales, for most SMBs | Marketing-mix models need 2–3 years of weekly data across several channels | Offer acquisition-cost and payback arithmetic plus a planned test, not a claim about cause and effect |
| How sales respond to price, when prices have never changed | With no past price changes there is nothing to measure | Give break-even volume thresholds and recommend a short, controlled price test |
| Bad input data | Unreconciled books and unrecorded promotions corrupt any model | Open with a data-readiness score; the client confirms the data and assumptions in writing |
| New products, branches or markets | No history to learn from | Use similar products or the owner's own launch estimates, clearly marked as assumptions |
| AI arithmetic errors and made-up facts | Tests show AI models make numerical mistakes | Code calculates every figure; a checking script and a human check every number before delivery |
| No professional signature or credential | The founder is a beginner and not a registered accountant | A partner accountant signs where needed; present as a business analyst; never certify financial statements or advise on securities |
| Liability for decisions made using the report | Forecasts will sometimes be wrong | Engagement letter with liability capped at fees, no indirect losses, client-only use, disclosure of AI use; offer a free revision, never an accuracy guarantee |

That table becomes the outline of every report. Page one states the decisions in money and dates. A data-readiness score follows, then forecasts shown as ranges alongside how the method performed on past data, the scenario table, and a list of assumptions that the client signs. The report closes with a section titled "what this report cannot tell you," dated sources for every outside number, and a statement that AI tools were used and a human reviewed the output. This honesty is not only good ethics. It is what sets the service apart from free chatbots, which present guesses with confidence.

## Conclusion

The research turns the original idea around. The instinct is that AI's ability to predict is the product. The evidence says prediction is the cheapest and least reliable part. That is especially true in Egypt, where the 2024 float and the 2026 cost shock driven by oil prices make single-number forecasts weakest exactly when owners most need guidance.

What keeps its value is disciplined scenario thinking. That means turning economic uncertainty into a few explicit paths, linking each to a dated effect on cash and margin, and standing behind the explanation. This is a human-plus-code service that Claude speeds up rather than replaces. It also suits a business-analysis graduate better than a race to build the best model, because the Excel smoothing and regression methods those courses teach are the ones that work on small-firm data.

Timing matters as much as the product. E-invoicing has just made Egyptian SME sales data machine-readable, and will do the same for Saudi and UAE firms during 2027. Accounting software makers and well-funded Gulf companies are only starting to build forecasting into their tools. In that window, what a solo founder can build that lasts is not code. It is distribution and reliable judgement: relationships with accountants and software installers, a library of case studies, and a report format clients trust.

If those exist when the platform is built, the platform becomes the behind-the-scenes engine those partners use for their Arabic-speaking SMEs. Fathom already proves that model in the West by charging accountants per client company. If they do not exist, no amount of AI will make up for them.
