*Method note for the report writer: the sandbox's network policy blocked direct page fetches (WebFetch/curl got DNS or proxy 403 errors on oecd.org, daftra.com, sharedserviceslink.com and others). ## 1. How many SMEs are there in Egypt, Saudi Arabia and the UAE, by size and sector?

### Takeaway
Egypt has roughly 3.7–3.8 million establishments. Over 99% of them have fewer than 50 employees, about 92% are micro, and about half are informal. But the newest official census data is from 2017/18, and a 6th census with a 2022 reference year has not visibly published results. Saudi Arabia has about 1.7 million active commercial registrations (Q2 2025), and the UAE about 557,000 SMEs (end-2022) within 1.2 million+ companies. The reachable, formal, data-holding segment in Egypt is far smaller than the headline count.

### Cited Findings
**Egypt: counts and structure**
- CAPMAS's 5th Economic Census (2017/18) is the main source of SME statistics. It reports about 3.74 million establishments, almost all private sector, with about 1,500 public/public-business-sector establishments — [CAPMAS census results](https://censusinfo.capmas.gov.eg/Metadata-en-v4.2/index.php/catalog/405/download/840); [OECD, SME and Entrepreneurship Policy in Egypt](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/sme-and-entrepreneurship-policy-in-egypt_195d9c43-en/full-report/overall-assessment-and-recommendations_99e00bc1.html)
- Per the OECD reading of the census, about 99.7% of establishments had fewer than 50 employees and 91.7% were micro-enterprises. About 53% of establishments were unregistered (informal) in 2017 — [OECD Egypt SME chapter](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/sme-and-entrepreneurship-policy-in-egypt_195d9c43-en/full-report/sme-and-entrepreneurship-performance-and-characteristics-in-egypt_a963c2a5.html)
- OECD summaries give slightly different headline totals: 3.7 million establishments active in 2017 in one place, and 3.8 million micro enterprises plus 67,600 small and medium enterprises in another. The census is not broken down by the MSME Law 152/2020 definitions, so no official count under the legal definition exists — [OECD Egypt SME chapter](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/sme-and-entrepreneurship-policy-in-egypt_195d9c43-en/full-report/sme-and-entrepreneurship-performance-and-characteristics-in-egypt_a963c2a5.html)
- CAPMAS said it would launch the 6th Economic Census before the end of FY2024/25. It is to include data on e-commerce, outsourcing and the green economy — [Amwal Al Ghad](https://en.amwalalghad.com/?p=192712). The CAPMAS preparation document sets calendar 2022 as the private-sector reference period — [CAPMAS document](https://censusinfo.capmas.gov.eg/Metadata-ar-v4.2/index.php/catalog/1948/download/7078). I found no published results.
- **Legal size definitions (Law 152/2020 as published):** micro = turnover under EGP 1m; small = EGP 1m to under 50m; medium = EGP 50m–200m. New firms are classed by capital — [Law No. 152 of 2020 (Andersen copy)](https://eg.andersen.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Law-No.-152-of-2020.pdf). The Cabinet approved amendments raising the bands: micro under EGP 2m, small EGP 2m to under 100m, medium EGP 100m–400m, with the minister able to adjust by up to 50%. Whether the amendment is in force is unconfirmed — [Amwal Al Ghad](https://en.amwalalghad.com/?p=225404)
- **Manufacturing:** the Federation of Egyptian Industries has 19 industrial chambers representing about 104,000 industrial establishments (about 90% private) employing 2 million+ workers. These are self-reported profile figures, and other profiles say 102,000 or 60,000 — [Union of Arab Chambers profile](https://uac-org.org/en/arabchamber/affiliated-members/federation-of-egyptian-industries); [AIM Congress](https://aimcongress.com/partner/federation-of-egyptian-industries-fei); [Amwal Al Ghad](https://en.amwalalghad.com/?p=47988)
- **Firm performance (World Bank Enterprise Survey, about 1,000 firms, Sep 2025–Jan 2026, data released May 2026):** real annual sales fell 11.4%, against +5.2% for MENA. Firms self-fund about 88% of capital spending, and banks fund about 3% (MENA: 15%). Only 3.2% of small firms and 1.4% of large firms use bank finance for investment, against 24.7% of medium firms. About 16% name electricity as their top obstacle — [EnterpriseAM, 1 Jun 2026](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2026/06/01/egyptian-firms-bypass-banks-to-fund-growth-from-their-own-pockets/); [WBES data updates](https://www.enterprisesurveys.org/en/data/data-updates); [WB microdata catalog, Egypt ES 2025](https://microdata.worldbank.org/catalog/8255)

**Saudi Arabia**
- Monsha'at's SME Monitor for Q2 2025 counts about 1.7 million active commercial registrations, with 80,000+ new ones in the quarter. Riyadh had 35.2% of new registrations and Makkah 18.1%. 47% are female-owned and 38% youth-owned. There are 39,366 e-commerce registrations — [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/gcc/monshaat-report-saudi-commercial-registrations-hit-17mln-hxxoyd1b); [Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/culture-society/saudi-startups-raise-862m-as-80000-new-smes-launched-in-q2-2025)
- Earlier readings: 1.5 million in Q2 2024 and 1.6 million in Q4 2024 — [Monsha'at Q4 2024](https://monshaat.gov.sa/en/node/274250); [Monsha'at Q2 2024](https://monshaat.gov.sa/en/node/169130)

**UAE**
- The UAE had 557,000 SMEs at end-2022 (target: 1 million by 2030). SMEs make up about 95% of companies, 86% of the private-sector workforce and 63.5% of non-oil GDP (mid-2022 figure) — [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/hszrgtp9-557000-smes-operated-uae-end-2022-minister)
- In September 2025 the Minister of Economy said the UAE hosts 1.2 million+ companies, about 94% (around 1 million) owned by entrepreneurs, and targets 2 million+ by 2031 — [WAM, Sep 2025](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/bltjo32-minister-economy-uae-seeks-increase-number)

### Inferences
- Egypt's 3.7m+ figure is mostly informal micro-units (kiosks, workshops, small shops) that have no structured data and little cash for reports. The realistic paying segment is more likely tens of thousands of formal small and medium firms, for example the ~104k FEI-affiliated industrial establishments plus formal retail, F&B, healthcare and e-commerce firms. No source gives this number directly.
- The World Bank 2025/26 survey (sales down 11.4% in real terms, investment self-funded) points to cash-constrained firms. They need cash-flow and pricing decisions badly, but have little discretionary budget.
- Saudi and UAE SME counts are each smaller than Egypt's but far more formalised and higher-revenue per firm.

### Gaps
- No official 2023–2026 count of Egyptian SMEs by MSME-law size band or sector. The 6th Economic Census results were not found.
- No MSMEDA-published SME count was found.
- No 2026 Monsha'at SME Monitor figures were found (latest found: Q2 2025). A "1.3 million SMEs at end-2023" Monsha'at figure appeared in search snippets, but I could not confirm the source URL.
- No post-2022 official UAE SME count.

## 2. Digital and data maturity: what usable historical data do typical Egyptian SMEs have?

### Takeaway
Egypt's tax digitisation (B2B e-invoicing since 2020–22, B2C e-receipts rolled out in waves through 2025) is creating structured, machine-readable sales data for formal firms. The Law 6/2025 simplified regime (turnover up to EGP 20m) requires e-invoice enrolment. Separately, only about half of SMEs take digital payments. No reliable statistic exists on accounting-software or ERP use among Egyptian SMEs. Historic evidence shows only about 8–14% of micro and small household enterprises kept regular books. Saudi Arabia and the UAE are more digitised and have e-invoicing mandates that now reach small firms (KSA) or arrive in 2027 (UAE).

### Cited Findings
**Egypt: tax e-invoicing and e-receipts (structured data)**
- The Egyptian Tax Authority's e-invoicing platform had processed more than 1.5 billion electronic documents by May 2025 (per the ETA head, Rasha Abdel Aal). E-invoice enrolment is a prerequisite for the simplified regime under Law No. 6 of 2025 — [Shared Services Link, May 2025](https://sharedserviceslink.com/news/egypt-s-e-invoicing-system-surpasses-1-5-billion-documents-)
- E-invoicing started in 2020/2022 with 134 large companies, then 400 more, then mandatory waves. The e-receipt (B2C) pilot began in 2022, with wider expansion from 2023 — [Edicom](https://edicomgroup.com/electronic-invoicing/egypt); [VATit](https://vatit.com/blog/e-invoicing-in-egypt/)
- **B2C e-receipt waves:** an ETA announcement of 26 July 2025 obliged new taxpayers to issue e-receipts from 15 September 2025 (Decision 281/2025) — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2025/07/tnf-egypt-new-taxpayers-required-to-comply-with-mandate-to-issue-electronic-receipts-for-b2c-transactions.html). Decision 361 set a 15 November 2025 start for a named list, described as the first sub-phase of the ninth phase. There is no universal turnover threshold: obligation is by named lists, and the EGP 150,000 figure in the Receipt v1.2 spec is about buyer identification — [Invoice Data Extraction guide](https://invoicedataextraction.com/blog/egypt-electronic-receipt-compliance-guide). Sources disagree on phase labels — [SNI Technology](https://snitechnology.net/?p=14064)
- **Law 6 of 2025 simplified regime:** effective 1 March 2025, for businesses with annual turnover up to EGP 20m, including unregistered firms. Tax is turnover-based at 0.4% (up to EGP 500k) to 1.5% (EGP 10–20m) instead of 22.5% on profit, with audit exemptions — [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/egypt-introduces-tax-incentives-and-benefits-for-small-enterprises); [Beancount guide, Jul 2026](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/26/egypt-law-6-2025-sme-simplified-tax-regime-guide). The ETA says the regime is permanent, allows quarterly instead of monthly VAT returns, and does not require complex accounting records — [ETA (Arabic)](https://eta.gov.eg/ar/news/alnzam-aldryby-almbst-wfqa-llqanwn-rqm-6-lsnt-2025-frst-daymt-ldm-almnshat-alsghyrt-wthqyq); [Daily News Egypt](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/?p=851383)
- Odoo Middle East markets an Egypt localisation supporting mandatory e-invoicing (vendor claim) — [Zawya press release](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/companies-news/odoo-middle-east-to-support-mandatory-e-invoicing-in-egypt-n1md7zkz)

**Egypt: payments, POS and software**
- A Visa and MSMEDA study (July 2025; 270 SME owners and managers, with 4Sight Research) found 53% of merchants adopted digital payments in the last two years. 55% of cash-only merchants are interested, 77% say digital payments are crucial to growth, and 61% of adopters plan more investment (cards 45%, wallets 36%). SMEs want value-added services, including **"access to data-driven insights,"** loyalty programmes and security. Cash reliance and low digital awareness persist — [Visa newsroom](https://www.visa.com/en-eg/newsroom/egypt-smes-digital-payments-growth-study); [Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/553109.aspx); [MEA Tech Watch](https://meatechwatch.com/2025/07/30/visa-and-msmeda-highlight-egypts-growing-digital-payments-among-smes/)
- Paymob serves about 390,000–395,000 merchants across Egypt, the UAE, KSA and Oman, with no country split. It had 350,000+ in 2024 and targeted 450,000 in Egypt by 2025 (unconfirmed) — [Forbes ME Fintech 50 2025](https://www.forbesmiddleeast.com/lists/the-middle-easts-fintech-50-2025/paymob/); [TechCrunch 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/11/paymob-lands-another-22-million-and-is-profitable-in-egypt); [Wamda, Sep 2026 pre-Series C](https://www.wamda.com/2026/09/paymob-raises-35-million-pre-series-c-led-mubadala-ebrd)
- Foodics (restaurant POS) serves 40,000+ branches across KSA, the UAE, Egypt, Jordan and Kuwait, with no Egypt split. Its Egypt business grew more than 3x year on year at its third anniversary (2023). Egyptian clients include cloud kitchens, dine-in, bakeries, QSRs and franchises — [Forbes ME](https://www.forbesmiddleeast.com/lists/the-middle-easts-fintech-50/foodics/); [Foodics press](https://www.foodics.com/press/foodics-celebrates-3-years-in-egypt/)
- Only 1.7% of Egyptian websites use a CRM system, and Odoo leads with 31.3% of those. This is a web-tech proxy, not a firm survey — [WMTips](https://www.wmtips.com/technologies/crm/country/eg/)
- **E-commerce:** estimates conflict widely, from about $2.34bn in 2025 revenue ([ECDB](https://ecommercedb.com/markets/eg/all)) to about $10.2bn in 2025 rising to $19.3bn by 2030 ([MEA Tech Watch](https://meatechwatch.com/?p=31581)). Noon is the largest online retailer. WooCommerce leads store platforms, followed by Shopify, Magento and others.

**Egypt: record-keeping (historic)**
- In ELMPS household-enterprise data, about 8.4% kept regular book accounts in 1998 and about 14.3% in 2006 (weighted, first economic unit). The portal cautions these are indicative — [ERF portal 1998](https://mail.erfdataportal.com/index.php/catalog/28/variable/V5248); [ERF portal 2006](https://erfdataportal.com/index.php/catalog/27/variable/V16431). An ERF paper finds that progress toward formalisation of micro and small enterprises reversed between 2006 and 2012 — [Rashed & Sieverding, ERF WP 831](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:erg:wpaper:831). **Old data, flag.**

**Saudi Arabia and UAE**
- **KSA ZATCA Phase 2 (integration):** Wave 25 (announced 24 July 2026) covers taxpayers with VAT-taxable revenue above **SAR 187,500** in any year 2022–2025, with a 1 February 2027 deadline. Wave 24 (revenue above SAR 375k) had a 30 June 2026 deadline — [EY, Aug 2026](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2026-1705-saudi-arabia-announces-25th-wave-of-phase-2-e-invoicing-integration); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/07/saudi-arabia-e-invoicing-mandatory-for-taxpayers-with-revenue-above-sar-187500.html); [Comarch](https://www.comarch.com/trade-and-services/data-management/legal-regulation-changes/saudi-arabia-announces-wave-25-of-e-invoicing-integration/). Fines reportedly start at SAR 5,000 — [Wafeq](https://www.wafeq.com/en-sa/tax-and-reporting/which-zatca-wave-am-i-in-deadlines-and-what-to-do)
- **UAE e-invoicing:** voluntary or pilot from 1 July 2026. Mandatory go-live is 1 January 2027 for revenue of AED 50m+ (service provider appointed by 30 October 2026) and 1 July 2027 for those under AED 50m. It applies regardless of VAT registration, and B2C is not yet included — [KPMG, Jul 2026](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/07/tnf-uae-phased-implementation-of-e-invoicing-beginning-january-1-2027.html); [Cleartax](https://www.cleartax.com/ksa/e-invoicing-united-arab-emirates); [Fonoa](https://www.fonoa.com/resources/blog/uae-national-electronic-invoicing-system-rollout)

### Inferences
- The best data assets for a forecasting product in Egypt are: (a) e-invoice/e-receipt histories, held in the ETA portal or in the ERP/POS that issues them; (b) POS exports (Foodics, Odoo POS, Daftra); (c) payment-gateway transaction logs (Paymob, Fawry, etc.); (d) e-commerce order exports (WooCommerce, Shopify). Firms on e-invoicing since 2022–23 may have 2–4 years of transaction-level history, which spans the March 2024 float. That history is valuable for modelling, but it is also a structural break.
- Visa/MSMEDA's finding that SMEs want "data-driven insights" from their payment tools is direct evidence of demand for analytics in the payment-adopting half of SMEs.
- The Law 6/2025 regime drops the need for complex books, so many small firms will have sales data (e-invoices) but weak cost and margin data. Forecasting reports may need to rely on revenue and transaction data plus owner-supplied cost assumptions.
- In KSA, the SAR 187,500 threshold means nearly all VAT-registered SMEs will have integrated e-invoice data by early 2027. That makes Saudi SMEs data-rich.

### Gaps
- No credible survey of the share of Egyptian SMEs using accounting software or ERP (Odoo, Zoho, Daftra, QuickBooks) versus Excel or paper. I found none from CAPMAS, ITIDA, MSMEDA or vendors.
- No current count of taxpayers registered on Egypt's e-invoice or e-receipt systems. Arabic press mentioned "24 million+ receipts" with no date or verifiable source.
- No count of Egyptian online merchants, and no Shopify or Bosta Egypt figures.

## 3. Economic context 2023–2026: inflation, devaluation, rates, imports, and SME planning pain

### Takeaway
Egyptian SMEs have been through a 2022–24 currency crisis and the 6 March 2024 float, when EGP moved from about 30.9 to about 50 per USD. Policy rates peaked at 27.25% in 2024. In 2025–26 inflation and rates came down (urban inflation 13.9% in September 2026; overnight deposit rate about 19% after the February 2026 cut). But 2026 brought a new cost shock from regional conflict and oil above $100: fuel prices rose 14–17% in March 2026, input costs rose at their fastest pace since January 2023, and the PMI has stayed below 50. Volatile costs, falling real sales and almost no bank finance make forecasting and pricing decisions more valuable, and also harder, because history contains structural breaks.

### Cited Findings
**Currency**
- On 6 March 2024 the CBE floated the pound. The rate settled near EGP 50/USD from about 30.9 (a ~62% move) and closed the parallel-market gap. Before the float, importers faced cash-cover rules and port backlogs — [FrontierView](https://frontierview.com/?p=55205)
- The pound passed 50/USD again in December 2024 — [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/investing/2024/12/05/egypt-pound-falls-past-50-per-dollar-for-first-time-since-march/). It traded at about EGP 47.2–47.7/USD in early January 2026 — [CNBC Arabia](https://www.cnbcarabia.com/147290/2026/06/01/سعر-صرف-الدولار-مقابل-الجنيه-المصري-اليوم-الثلاثاء-6-يناير-2026). Undated 2026 bank quotes show about 47.9–48.4 — [Alyaum](https://www.alyaum.com/ampArticle/6540849). **No verified October 2026 rate found.**
- In the 2022 float, some importers ended up with bank debt far above equity — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/business%2Fegyptian-importers-face-bankruptcy-after-currency-float-1.1957880). AUC analysis notes that locally made goods rose 25–50% because their inputs are imported (2022 episode) — [AUC Alternative Policy Solutions](https://api-aps.aucegypt.edu//uploads/articlesAttachments/1669200317pdffinal.pdf). **Older episodes, flag.**

**Interest rates**
- The overnight deposit rate was 19.25% at end-2023 and 27.25% at end-2024, after 800bp of hikes in 2024 — [Focus Economics](https://focus-economics.com/country-indicator/egypt/interest-rate). It was held at 27.25% in February 2025 — [Egypt Today](https://www.egypttoday.com/Article/3/138560/Egypt’s-central-bank-keeps-interest-rates-unchanged-during-its-first). Cuts followed: −225bp to 25% (April 2025, [Aletihad](https://en.aletihad.ae/news/business/4567476/egypt-cuts-key-policy-rates-by-225-basis-points)); −200bp to 22% (August 2025, [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/15qs9kj-egypt’s-central-bank-cuts-interest-rates)); −100bp to 21% (October 2025, [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/egypt/interest-rate/news/525411)); a hold in November 2025; −100bp to 20% (December 2025).
- In February 2026 the CBE cut 100bp to 19% and lowered reserve requirements. Urban inflation was 11.9% in January 2026 — [Central Banking](https://www.centralbanking.com/central-banks/monetary-policy/monetary-policy-decisions/7975038/egypt-cuts-rates-by-100bp-and-lowers-reserve-requirements); [Business Today Egypt](https://www.businesstodayegypt.com/Article/1/7140/Egypt’s-central-bank-cuts-key-interest-rates-by-100-bps). An early-October 2026 preview said the rate was still 19%, with the next MPC on 29 October 2026 — [Daba Finance](https://dabafinance.com/fr/nouvelles/egypt-central-bank-rate-cut-inflation-2026) (secondary).

**Inflation 2026**
- Urban headline inflation was 13.9% in September 2026, down from 14.5% in August, a seven-month low. Month-on-month inflation rose 1.3%, with vegetables up 15.1%. Nationwide inflation was 12.8% and rural 11.6% — [Daily News Egypt, 10 Oct 2026](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/2026/10/10/egypts-annual-urban-inflation-eases-to-13-9-in-september/). Core inflation was 14.1% (from 14.9%) — [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/egypts-annual-core-inflation-eases-to-14.1-in-september-cbe-1589028)
- Fuel prices rose about 14–17% in early March 2026 amid high global oil prices. Transport inflation is about 24%, and housing and utilities 35.2% — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/egypt/inflation-cpi/news/590967)
- The Arab Monetary Fund expects Egyptian inflation of about 17% in 2026, citing regional conflict and oil passing $100/bbl for the first time since August 2022 — [Al-Ahram Gate (Arabic)](https://gate.ahram.org.eg/News/5871169.aspx). One secondary source says the CBE expects 16–17% average 2026 inflation (vs 14.1% in 2025). That is unverified and sits uneasily with 12–14% monthly prints — [Erem Business](https://www.erembusiness.com/economy/l4gikqr). The CBE's target is 7% ±2pp on average in Q4 2026, and it now sees the target reached in H2 2027 — [Daily News Egypt](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/2026/10/10/egypts-annual-urban-inflation-eases-to-13-9-in-september/)

**Activity (PMI) and the IMF**
- The S&P Global Egypt PMI was 47.2 in September 2026, down from 49.6 in August, with sharp falls in new orders and output. Purchasing has contracted for 6 months and inventories for 3. Firms raised selling prices strongly — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/egypt/manufacturing-pmi/news/589380). In April–May 2026 input-cost inflation was the fastest since January 2023 (fuel, electricity, currency, wages). June's 46.0 was the weakest in about 3.5 years — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/egypt/manufacturing-pmi/news/547684); [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/egypt/manufacturing-pmi/news/555974)
- On 30 July 2026 the IMF completed its 7th EFF review, releasing about $1.8bn. Growth was 5.2% in the first nine months of FY2025/26, with about 4.6% expected for the full year and a current-account deficit of about 4.5% of GDP. The IMF calls for a market-determined exchange rate and tight monetary policy — [IMF PR 26/271](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/07/30/pr26271-egypt-imf-completes-the-7th-review-under-eff-and-2nd-review-under-the-rsf); [Arab Finance](https://arabfinance.com/en/news/newdetails/imf-unlocks-fresh-financing-for-egypt-after-completion-of-seventh-eff-review). The EFF expires 15 December 2026, and the final review (about $2.3bn) is expected in Q4 2026 — [ET Business View](https://etbusinessview.com/2026/10/02/imf-says-egypts-final-eff-review-expected-in-fourth-quarter/)

**SME finance and planning pain**
- The World Bank Enterprise Survey 2025/26 found real sales down 11.4%, about 88% of investment self-funded, and bank finance about 3% — [EnterpriseAM](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2026/06/01/egyptian-firms-bypass-banks-to-fund-growth-from-their-own-pockets/)
- Since 2021 the CBE has required banks to lend 25% of their loan portfolios to MSMEs, at least 10% to small firms — [Enterprise](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2021/02/23/msmes-will-have-an-easier-time-getting-bank-finance-thanks-to-the-cbe/). MSMEDA disbursed about EGP 6bn in 2024 and planned to double that in 2025 — [Business Today Egypt](https://www.businesstodayegypt.com/amp/1/5837/MSMEDA-invests-EGP-6B-into-MSME-sector-in-2024-plans)
- Anecdote (February 2025): an Alexandria music-shop owner went from selling about 10 guitars a month to 1–2 after the pound weakened — [The Click](https://theclick.news/two-small-business-owners-in-alexandria-hope-for-a-brighter-future)

### Inferences
- The forecasting need is acute. Costs move monthly (food, fuel, electricity, FX), demand is weakening (PMI below 50, real sales falling), and firms self-fund, so cash-flow mistakes are existential. Repricing frequency, inventory sizing and FX pass-through are concrete decision problems a report can address.
- The modelling challenge is real. Data spanning 2022–2024 contains multiple regime shifts (floats, rate swings of 800bp up and about 825bp down). Naive time-series forecasts will mislead. Reports should use scenarios (FX at 45/50/55, inflation paths) and real-terms (EGP-deflated or USD) analysis rather than single-point forecasts.
- Banks must lend 25% to MSMEs, yet SMEs barely use bank finance. That gap suggests bank-ready cash-flow forecasts and feasibility studies are a practical selling point, since banks and MSMEDA ask for feasibility studies.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 survey measuring Egyptian SME pricing behaviour, margins or cash-flow distress specifically. I found only macro data and anecdotes.
- No verified October 2026 EGP/USD rate.
- The CBE's own 2026 average-inflation forecast is unverified (conflicting secondary reports).

## 4. Willingness and ability to pay for advisory and analytics in Egypt

### Takeaway
Price points in Egypt are low. Commercial accounting offices quote roughly EGP 3,000–7,000 a month for small companies. Arabic freelance marketplaces sell basic feasibility studies from about $25. SME SaaS like Daftra starts at about $10/month. Gulf price points are an order of magnitude higher: fractional CFO work runs SAR 3,000–15,000 a month in KSA, and a UAE feasibility study is quoted at AED 15,000+. Egyptian SMEs mostly self-fund, and cash use persists among the half not yet on digital payments.

### Cited Findings
- **Accountants (Egypt):** commercial accounting offices typically charge about EGP 3,000–7,000 a month for small companies, depending on transaction volume. This comes from marketing pages, not a fee survey — [Zad Agency (Arabic, 2026)](https://zadagency.com/%D9%83%D9%85-%D8%AA%D9%83%D9%84%D9%81%D8%A9-%D9%85%D9%83%D8%AA%D8%A8-%D9%85%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D9%81%D9%89-2026/%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%AA%D8%B4%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D9%85%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%A9/); [Wefaq Group](https://wefaqgroup.com/%D9%85%D9%83%D8%AA%D8%A8-%D9%85%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%A8-%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86%D9%8A-%D9%81%D9%89-%D9%85%D8%B5%D8%B1-2025/)
- **Feasibility studies (دراسة جدوى):** gigs on Khamsat (an Arabic freelance marketplace) list preliminary feasibility studies from about **$25** (for example a 20-page financial study, or a 5–8 page study with Excel). Sellers state these are preliminary and decision-oriented, not accredited for lenders — [Khamsat listing](https://khamsat.com/business/feasibility-study/4472117-%D8%AF%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%A9-%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%89-%D9%85%D8%A8%D8%AF%D8%A6%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B9%D9%83-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B5%D8%BA%D9%8A%D8%B1-%D8%A8%D8%A3%D8%B1%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%85-%D9%85%D9%88%D8%AB%D9%82%D8%A9-%D9%88%D9%85%D9%84%D9%81-excel); [Khamsat listing](https://khamsat.com/business/feasibility-study/2992497-%D8%AF%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%A9-%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%8A-%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%85%D9%81%D8%B5%D9%84%D8%A9-%D9%84%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%B9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B5%D8%BA%D9%8A%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D9%88-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AA%D9%88%D8%B3%D8%B7%D8%A9). Egypt also has "accredited" feasibility offices that serve bank and investment-authority submissions — [Projects Egypt](https://projectsegy.com/accredited-feasibility-studies-offices-egypt/) (no prices found).
- **UAE feasibility study:** said to cost no less than AED 15,000 (low-quality content-site source) — [small-projects.org (Arabic)](https://small-projects.org/%D8%A3%D8%B3%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%AF%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%89-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA)
- **SME SaaS:** Daftra's Egypt page lists monthly tiers of $9.99, $19.95 and $40, or $120, $239 and $480 a year, with extra users at $6 — [Daftra hub page](https://www.daftra.com/en/hub/best-accounting-software-in-egypt). TrustRadius lists different tiers ($30, $55, $70) — [TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/daftra/pricing). The conflict is unresolved, and no EGP price list was found.
- **KSA fractional CFO:** SAR 3,000–15,000 a month depending on scope, with fundraising and restructuring projects priced separately. This is a marketplace estimate from an accounting-software vendor — [Wafeq CFO directory](https://www.wafeq.com/en-sa/accounting-partners/directory/cfo-services)
- **Freelance analyst rates (Egypt):** I found only individual asking prices, for example a Cairo data analyst at €150/day on Malt and another at $5/hr on Hubstaff Talent. These are not market benchmarks — [Malt profile](https://en.malt.de/profile/mostafafathy2); [Hubstaff Talent](https://hubstafftalent.net/profiles/amir-shehata)
- **Payment habits:** 53% of SMEs have adopted digital payments, cash reliance persists, and 41% cite theft fears linked to cash — [Visa/MSMEDA via MEA Tech Watch](https://meatechwatch.com/2025/07/30/visa-and-msmeda-highlight-egypts-growing-digital-payments-among-smes/). Firms fund about 88% of investment internally — [EnterpriseAM](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2026/06/01/egyptian-firms-bypass-banks-to-fund-growth-from-their-own-pockets/)

### Inferences
- At about EGP 48/USD, an accountant retainer of EGP 3,000–7,000 a month is roughly $60–150. A one-off Egyptian SME forecasting report probably has to land at around EGP 2,500–10,000 ($50–200) to sit beside these anchors. Recurring monthly "forecast refresh" pricing should sit below or near the accountant retainer. These are inferences, not survey data.
- The $25 feasibility-study gigs set a very low anchor for generic documents. Differentiation has to come from using the client's own transaction data and scenario modelling (FX and inflation), and ideally a bank-ready format.
- Gulf clients can plausibly pay 5–20x Egyptian prices: SAR 3,000–15,000 a month is about $800–4,000, and AED 15,000 is about $4,100 for a feasibility study.

### Gaps
- No systematic survey of Egyptian consultant, auditor or feasibility-office fees. Prices are mostly from marketing pages.
- No Egyptian SME willingness-to-pay study for analytics or BI.
- No EGP pricing for Odoo, Zoho or QuickBooks in Egypt, and the Daftra price conflict is unresolved.
- No reliable freelance-rate survey for Egyptian financial modellers or data analysts. An ECES 2024 working paper compares Egyptian and Indian freelancer earnings, but its findings could not be accessed — [ECES WP 236](https://eces.org.eg/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/W-P-En-236.pdf)

## 5. Sectors with the best fit

### Takeaway
The best early fits are sectors where transaction data already exists digitally (POS, e-receipts, e-commerce, payment logs) and where inflation and FX shocks hit decisions directly. That points to restaurants and F&B (POS-rich), modern retail and e-commerce sellers, pharmacies and distributors (FX-sensitive, inventory-heavy), and import-dependent manufacturers. Direct sector-level adoption data is thin, so this ranking rests mainly on inference.

### Cited Findings
- **F&B:** Foodics serves 40,000+ branches across KSA, the UAE, Egypt, Jordan and Kuwait. Its Egyptian clients span cloud kitchens, dine-in, bakeries, QSRs and franchises, and its Egypt business more than tripled year on year by 2023 — [Forbes ME](https://www.forbesmiddleeast.com/lists/the-middle-easts-fintech-50/foodics/); [Foodics press](https://www.foodics.com/press/foodics-celebrates-3-years-in-egypt/). Vegetable prices rose 15.1% in a single month (September 2026), showing the input-cost volatility restaurants face — [Daily News Egypt](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/2026/10/10/egypts-annual-urban-inflation-eases-to-13-9-in-september/)
- **Retail and B2C:** B2C e-receipt mandates have been extended through waves in 2025 (September and November 2025 lists) and create receipt-level sales histories — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2025/07/tnf-egypt-new-taxpayers-required-to-comply-with-mandate-to-issue-electronic-receipts-for-b2c-transactions.html); [Invoice Data Extraction](https://invoicedataextraction.com/blog/egypt-electronic-receipt-compliance-guide)
- **E-commerce:** e-commerce is growing 20–25% a year (ECDB), and mobile accounted for 72% of 2024 sales — [ECDB](https://ecommercedb.com/markets/eg/all); [MEA Tech Watch](https://meatechwatch.com/?p=31581). In KSA there are 39,366 e-commerce registrations (Q2 2025) — [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/gcc/monshaat-report-saudi-commercial-registrations-hit-17mln-hxxoyd1b)
- **Pharma and pharmacies:** Egypt's pharmaceutical market was about EGP 422bn (about $8.5bn) in 2025, with about 4bn units. Ibnsina Pharma held about 35% of the pharmacy and wholesale segment in H1 2025 (IQVIA), and that segment is about 69% of the drug market — [Arab Finance](https://arabfinance.com/en/news/newdetails/29011) (figure from search summary; verify). EnterpriseAM covers pharma "expansion amid price hikes and regulatory shifts" — [EnterpriseAM](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/inside-industry/pharma-sector-expands-amid-price-hikes-and-regulatory-shifts/)
- **Manufacturing:** about 104,000 FEI-affiliated industrial establishments — [UAC profile](https://uac-org.org/en/arabchamber/affiliated-members/federation-of-egyptian-industries). Input costs rose at their fastest since January 2023 in April–May 2026 on fuel, electricity and materials, and purchasing and inventories are contracting — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/egypt/manufacturing-pmi/news/589380). Locally made goods rise 25–50% after floats because inputs are imported (2022 data) — [AUC APS](https://api-aps.aucegypt.edu//uploads/articlesAttachments/1669200317pdffinal.pdf)
- **Real estate:** Flat6Labs and Cityscape Egypt are running the third Egypt PropTech Challenge in 2026, a sign of active proptech and developer ecosystem events — [Flat6Labs programs](https://flat6labs.com/programs-type/programs-for-entrepreneurs/)
- **Housing and utilities:** inflation of 35.2% and transport at about 24% (September 2026) hit clinics, logistics and any rent- or energy-heavy business — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/egypt/inflation-cpi/news/590967)

### Inferences
- **Tier 1 (data-rich and acute pain):** restaurants and cafés or multi-branch F&B on Foodics or Odoo POS (daily sales data; menu repricing; ingredient inflation); e-commerce sellers on WooCommerce or Shopify (order-level data; ad-spend and inventory decisions); distributors and wholesalers issuing B2B e-invoices (FX-linked purchasing, receivables and cash-flow forecasting).
- **Tier 2:** pharmacy chains (inventory-heavy, many SKUs, regulated prices with periodic hikes, though chains may already have analytics through distributors); import-dependent small manufacturers (pricing under input-cost shocks); exporters (FX upside and USD revenue, so better ability to pay).
- **Tier 3:** clinics (appointment data, but smaller decisions); real estate developers (high-value feasibility and pricing decisions with long-cycle data; they pay more, but competitors are established consultancies).
- New-project feasibility studies for bank, MSMEDA or investor applications are a distinct, recurring demand stream. Banks face the 25% MSME lending quota and MSMEDA disbursed about EGP 6bn in 2024.

### Gaps
- No sector-level data on SME digital or POS adoption in Egypt.
- No verified count of pharmacies (chain vs independent), restaurants or clinics in Egypt.
- No Egypt-specific Foodics, Shopify or Bosta merchant counts.

## 6. Channels to reach Egyptian SMEs with zero capital

### Takeaway
The cheapest scalable channels are: (1) intermediaries who already hold SME data and trust, namely accounting offices, ERP and POS implementers (Odoo partners, Daftra, Foodics) and e-invoice integrators; (2) public SME programmes (MSMEDA, ITIDA/TIEC, FEI chambers); (3) Facebook, where Egypt has about 60 million users, and LinkedIn, at about 18 million. Accelerators mostly serve tech startups, not traditional SMEs, so they are a secondary channel.

### Cited Findings
- **Facebook:** about 60.2 million Egyptian users in September 2026 (50.1% of population; 58.5% male). **LinkedIn:** about 18.07 million (15.1%), though the figure may be stale — [NapoleonCat, 2026](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/social-media-users-in-egypt/2026/). DataReportal counts 51.6 million social media user identities (October 2025, 43.4% of population) on a different methodology — [DataReportal Digital 2026: Egypt](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-egypt)
- **MSMEDA:** disbursed about EGP 6bn in 2024 (+13%) and planned to double in 2025 — [Business Today Egypt](https://www.businesstodayegypt.com/amp/1/5837/MSMEDA-invests-EGP-6B-into-MSME-sector-in-2024-plans). It has a regional reach, for example EGP 4.9bn to 128,290 projects in border governorates from 2014 to August 2025, and EGP 2.2bn to 51,000 projects in Alexandria — [Daily News Egypt](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/2025/10/15/msmeda-injects-egp-4-9bn-to-finance-128290-small-micro-projects-in-border-governorates-over-11-years/); [Amwal Al Ghad](https://en.amwalalghad.com/?p=209993). It partners with banks on SME lines, for example Banque Misr's EGP 300m facility for about 3,000 micro-projects (January 2025) — [Arab Finance](https://www.arabfinance.com/en/news/newdetails/21901). It co-authored the Visa SME payments study — [Visa](https://www.visa.com/en-eg/newsroom/egypt-smes-digital-payments-growth-study)
- **FEI:** 19 chambers and about 104k industrial establishments. The Federation of Egyptian Chambers of Commerce is a separate body (Law 6/2002) — [UAC FEI profile](https://uac-org.org/en/arabchamber/affiliated-members/federation-of-egyptian-industries); [UAC FEDCOC profile](https://uac-org.org/en/ArabChamber/arab-chambers/federation-of-egyptian-chambers-of-commerce)
- **ITIDA/TIEC:** the Invest-IT programme with Flat6Labs targets seed and pre-Series A tech startups. Start IT incubation offers up to EGP 480,000 (deadline 1 August 2026). An Upper Egypt bootcamp with Plug and Play supported 61 startups (closed March 2026) — [ITIDA](https://itida.gov.eg/English/PressReleases/Pages/itida-launches-invest-it-startup-funding-program-egypt.aspx); [Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/564937.aspx)
- **Flat6Labs:** active 2026 Egypt programme is the PropTech Challenge with Cityscape. Several earlier Egypt programmes are inactive — [Flat6Labs](https://flat6labs.com/programs-type/programs-for-entrepreneurs/). **Falak Startups:** I found only a 2024 listicle and could not confirm 2026 activity — [AcceleratorApp](https://www.acceleratorapp.co/en/blogs/top-startup-accelerators-incubators-cairo-egypt)
- **Accounting-partner ecosystems exist in the region:** for example, Wafeq runs a partner directory of accountants and CFO-service providers across KSA, the UAE, Bahrain and Oman — [Wafeq directory](https://www.wafeq.com/en-sa/accounting-partners/directory/cfo-services)
- Payment providers are moving into value-added data services. SMEs want "access to data-driven insights" beyond payments — [Visa/MSMEDA](https://www.visa.com/en-eg/newsroom/egypt-smes-digital-payments-growth-study)

### Inferences
- **Accountants as partners:** they already see client books and e-invoice data, charge retainers of EGP 3,000–7,000, and now face lighter compliance under Law 6/2025 (quarterly VAT, simpler records), which may squeeze their revenue. A white-label "forecast and scenario pack" they can upsell would be attractive. This is the highest-leverage zero-capital channel. (Inference.)
- **ERP, POS and e-invoice implementers** (Odoo partners, Daftra resellers, ETA integrators) sold compliance during 2022–2026 rollouts. Analytics on top of the newly structured data is a natural add-on for them.
- **Facebook groups** (Arabic-language merchant, e-commerce and restaurant-owner communities) reach far more people than LinkedIn in Egypt. LinkedIn suits medium firms, exporters and Gulf decision-makers.
- **MSMEDA and bank SME desks** need feasibility studies and cash-flow projections for loan applications. Offering "bank-ready" forecasts through these desks or their approved consultants is plausible, but no evidence was found of an open referral mechanism.

### Gaps
- No data on how many accounting or audit firms operate in Egypt, or on their openness to tech partnerships.
- No membership counts or SME-service catalogues for the Federation of Egyptian Chambers of Commerce.
- No verified 2026 information on EgyptInnovate, AUC Venture Lab or Falak SME (non-tech) programmes.
- No data on the size or engagement of Egyptian Facebook business groups.

## 7. Gulf (Saudi/UAE) SMEs as higher-paying remote clients for an Egypt-based provider

### Takeaway
Gulf SMEs are more formal, more digitised and pay several times Egyptian rates: fractional CFO work runs SAR 3,000–15,000 a month in KSA, and a UAE feasibility study is quoted at AED 15,000+. Mandatory e-invoicing, reaching KSA firms with revenue above SAR 187,500 by February 2027 and all UAE businesses by July 2027, will give them structured data. Egypt is already an established Arabic-language delivery hub for Gulf firms. The evidence for demand specifically for SME forecasting reports is indirect.

### Cited Findings
- **Market size:** about 1.7 million active Saudi commercial registrations (Q2 2025), growing about 80,000 a quarter — [Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/culture-society/saudi-startups-raise-862m-as-80000-new-smes-launched-in-q2-2025). 557k UAE SMEs (2022), with 1.2m+ companies in 2025 — [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/bltjo32-minister-economy-uae-seeks-increase-number)
- **Pricing:** KSA fractional CFO at SAR 3,000–15,000 a month — [Wafeq](https://www.wafeq.com/en-sa/accounting-partners/directory/cfo-services). UAE feasibility study at AED 15,000 or more (low-quality source) — [small-projects.org](https://small-projects.org/%D8%A3%D8%B3%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%AF%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%89-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA)
- **Data readiness:** ZATCA Wave 25 covers revenue above SAR 187,500 (connect by 1 February 2027) — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/07/saudi-arabia-e-invoicing-mandatory-for-taxpayers-with-revenue-above-sar-187500.html). UAE e-invoicing is mandatory from 1 January 2027 (AED 50m+) and 1 July 2027 (under AED 50m) — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/07/tnf-uae-phased-implementation-of-e-invoicing-beginning-january-1-2027.html)
- **Egypt as a Gulf delivery base:** firms serving Saudi and UAE customers already run regional call-centre and tech operations from Egypt on cost and talent grounds (per a Ziada DC executive) — [MEA Tech Watch](https://meatechwatch.com/?p=56790). Kuwait-based Alshaya opened its first offshoring Global Talent Centre in Cairo, inaugurated by ITIDA's CEO in April 2026 — [MEA Tech Watch](https://meatechwatch.com/2026/04/24/itida-ceo-inaugurates-alshaya-groups-first-offshoring-global-talent-center-in-cairo-marking-20-years-in-egypt/); [Egypt Independent](https://www.egyptindependent.com/itida-ceo-inaugurates-alshaya-groups-first-offshoring-global-talent-centre-in-cairo-marking-20-years-in-egypt/)
- Egypt's digital exports were $4.8bn in 2025, across 240+ offshoring companies and 270+ delivery centres (per Upwork's Egypt page) — [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/hire/eg/). This conflicts with a 2024 report of $6.2bn in 2023 and a ~$9bn 2026 target, and with a May 2026 ministerial target of $6bn — [Daily News Egypt](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/?p=852798); [Arab Finance (Arabic)](https://arabfinance.com/ar/news/newdetails/صادرات-رقمية-خلال-العام-الجاري) (unreconciled)
- **Payments infrastructure crossing borders:** Paymob holds a CBUAE retail payments licence (January 2025) and has onboarded about 20,000 GCC merchants since, indicating Egypt-origin fintech can serve Gulf SMEs — [Forbes ME](https://www.forbesmiddleeast.com/lists/the-middle-easts-fintech-50-2025/paymob/)

### Inferences
- An Egypt-based solo founder can price Gulf reports at about $300–1,500 per report, or $200–800 a month for monitoring, and still undercut local fractional CFOs ($800–4,000 a month). That margin is much higher than Egyptian pricing. (Inference from the pricing anchors above.)
- Arabic-native reporting, plus familiarity with GCC regulation (ZATCA, VAT, Saudisation context) and Arabic-language social channels, is a differentiator against Indian or Western offshore providers. Egypt's existing role as a Gulf back office supports credibility.
- The 2026–27 Gulf e-invoicing wave mirrors Egypt's 2022–25 rollout. The Egyptian founder's knowledge of turning e-invoice data into insight is directly transferable, and integrators and accountants handling ZATCA and UAE onboarding are potential channel partners.
- Saudi Arabia is likely the larger opportunity (bigger SME base, Arabic-first). The UAE has more English-language and multinational SMEs and more competition.

### Gaps
- No survey of Gulf SME demand for outsourced forecasting or analytics, or of their willingness to hire Egypt-based providers.
- No data on the share of Egypt's digital exports going to KSA or the UAE.
- No independent (non-vendor) price benchmarks for Gulf SME consulting or feasibility studies. The figures come from a vendor directory and a low-quality content site.
- Legal and payment frictions (contracting, invoicing Gulf clients from Egypt, receiving USD, VAT on imported services in KSA and the UAE) were not researched.
