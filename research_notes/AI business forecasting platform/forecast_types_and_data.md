## 1. Which forecasts matter most to SMBs (ranked catalog)

### Takeaway
No survey ranks forecast types for SMBs directly. The best indirect evidence, the Federal Reserve's Small Business Credit Survey and Intuit's cash-flow studies, puts cash (uneven cash flow, paying operating expenses, getting paid) and costs and pricing (rising costs, tariffs) at the top. Revenue/sales forecasting and budget/P&L projection are the base that every other analysis needs. Marketing-mix modelling and ML churn models come last because few SMBs have enough data for them.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Cash is the most painful and most frequent problem, and it is the one where a forecast turns directly into an action. It also needs the least history: about 13 weeks forward from current bank, AR, AP and payroll data, with no long time series. A solo founder should lead with it.
- Pricing and cost pass-through what-ifs are under-served and topical in 2025–2026 because of tariff and cost pressure (SBCS). The model is simple arithmetic on margins, so it is cheap to produce and easy to explain.
- Marketing MMM and ML churn are "valuable in theory, data-starved in practice" for most firms under 50 employees. They fit better as upsells for e-commerce or SaaS SMBs with clean digital data.
- Demand/inventory and staffing are vertical-specific. They are high value for retailers, wholesalers and restaurants and irrelevant for many service firms, so they suit vertical report templates.

### Gaps
- No survey found ranks forecast types for SMBs by importance or request frequency. The ranking above is inferred from problem-prevalence surveys and accountant advisory trends.
- The exact 2026 SBCS uneven-cash-flow percentage could not be extracted.
- No reliable survey was found on what share of SMBs produce any cash-flow forecast. The only figure is older RBS/NatWest UK data (see section 3).

## 2. Minimum data, data-quality problems, and methods per forecast type

### Takeaway
Most SMB analyses can run on data that already sits in accounting software (QuickBooks, Xero), bank feeds, and POS or e-commerce exports. The binding constraints are history length (seasonality needs multiple cycles), categorisation quality, and missing drivers such as price changes and promotions. There is no universal "24 months" rule: Hyndman argues the minimum depends on the number of model parameters and the noise in the data.

### Cited Findings
**General forecasting data and method principles**
- Hyndman and Kostenko (Foresight, 2007): "there are no simple rules about sample size." Published sample-size tables are oversimplified, meeting the minimums does not guarantee good seasonal estimates, and how much extra data is needed depends on noise. — [IDEAS/RePEc: Hyndman & Kostenko 2007](https://ideas.repec.org/a/for/ijafaa/y2007i6p12-15.html)
- Seasonality typically consumes m−1 degrees of freedom (m=12 for monthly data). Fourier terms are suggested when m is large relative to the sample size. The common "ARIMA needs 30 observations" rule is called a myth by a secondary source. — [Hyndman blog via R-bloggers, "Fitting models to short time series"](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2014/03/fitting-models-to-short-time-series/); [r-statistics.co](https://r-statistics.co/Forecasting-Short-Time-Series-in-R.html)
- M4 competition (100,000 series): 12 of the 17 most accurate methods were combinations of mostly statistical methods. The six pure ML entries did poorly: none beat the combination benchmark and only one beat Naïve2. The top method was a hybrid of exponential smoothing and an RNN, about 10% more accurate (sMAPE) than the combination benchmark. — [IIF/forecasters.org M4 press release](https://forecasters.org/blog/2018/06/19/the-m-competitions-and-their-far-reaching-contributions-to-the-theory-and-practice-of-forecasting); [M4 paper, IJF 34(4)](https://pure.unic.ac.cy/en/publications/the-m4-competition-results-findings-conclusion-and-way-forward/); [Uber M4 write-up](https://www.uber.com/at/en/blog/m4-forecasting-competition/)
- M5 competition (Walmart hierarchical retail unit sales): LightGBM-based approaches dominated the top entries, and the best methods cut error by more than one fifth against the most accurate benchmark. The winner averaged LightGBM models trained at different aggregation levels. — [Makridakis et al., M5 Accuracy paper (PDF)](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/M5_accuracy_competition.pdf); [ScienceDirect: M5 conclusions](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169207022000577). A later study found pooled LightGBM's advantage weakens after regime shifts, where ETS stays competitive — [arXiv 2310.17332](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.17332)
- FP&A Trends 2025 (secondary summary): 44% of organisations rate their forecast accuracy as very high or good. The figure is 77% for teams with fully driver-based models and 27% for basic models. — [FP&A Trends 2025 Survey PDF](https://fpa-trends.com/sites/default/files/docs/FPA-Trends-Survey-2025-OneStream.pdf); [FP&A Trends 2025 benchmarks article](https://fpa-trends.com/article/2025-fpa-benchmarks-and-trends)

**Per-type data and methods**

*A. Cash-flow forecast (13-week, direct method)*
- Inputs: reconciled opening bank balance at a fixed cut-off (not the ledger balance); AR aging, with receipts timed by expected payment date rather than invoice date and each customer's payment pattern applied to the current, 30–60 and 60–90 buckets; AP aging adjusted for actual payment behaviour such as batch runs; the payroll calendar, including months with three pay runs, plus employer taxes and benefits; rent, taxes, debt service, insurance, annual software renewals and capex. Discretionary lines such as marketing, travel and contractors are flagged as movable. The forecast is rolled weekly, with actuals replacing forecast, a new week 13 added, and variances reviewed. — [Centime 13-week guide](https://www.centime.com/articles/13-week-cash-flow-forecast); [Float 13-week guide](https://www.floatapp.com/blog/13-week-cash-flow-forecast); [Zone & Co NetSuite guide](https://www.zoneandco.com/articles/13-week-cash-flow-forecasting-netsuite); [Control-Fi template](https://control-fi.com/blog/13-week-cash-flow-forecast/)
- Practitioner advice: give every line a named data source and show uncertain receipts separately. Probability-weighted collections (e.g. 92% for current and 68% for 1–30 days past due) are illustrative, not benchmarks. — [HiData 13-week guide](https://hidata.ai/resources/13-week-cash-flow-forecast); [Numeric template](https://www.numeric.io/template/13-week-cash-flow-template)
- Built-in tools: Xero's cash-flow forecast covers 30, 60 or 180 days and dates cash from invoice due dates, while QuickBooks Online's cash flow planner draws on connected bank and card accounts (vendor-authored comparison). — [CXCash comparison](https://cxcash.com/blog/best-cash-flow-forecasting-software-small-business). A commentary piece argues ledger-native forecasts don't answer scenario questions such as hiring or losing a major client. — [Finoya blog](https://www.finoya.ai/blog/ledger-ai-cash-flow-limits/)
- Methods: rules-based schedule built from AR/AP timing, a fixed-cost calendar, and collection-lag assumptions. Statistical forecasting applies only to the variable receipts line (see the inference below).

*B. Sales/revenue and demand/inventory forecasting*
- Inventory: spreadsheets work for small catalogues with stable demand and break down as SKUs and channels grow. Vendors report stockout reductions of 20–50% with demand-planning tools, but these are vendor claims with no disclosed methodology. — [StockTrim](https://www.stocktrim.com/blog/which-inventory-forecasting-tool-is-best-for-smbs-in-2026); [inFlow: outgrown spreadsheets](https://www.inflowinventory.com/blog/spreadsheets-for-inventory/); [Finale Inventory](https://www.finaleinventory.com/guides/demand-planning-software-4/); [Cin7 forecasting guide](https://www.cin7.com/blog/inventory-forecasting/)
- Case example: Camif, a furniture e-commerce retailer of about 70 staff, moved procurement off spreadsheets and cut its stockout rate by 6 points (vendor case study). — [Flowlity](https://www.flowlity.com/resources/ai-powered-demand-planning-software-small-businesses)
- Methods in tools: Excel FORECAST.ETS needs a constant time step (otherwise it returns #NUM!), tolerates up to 30% missing points, fills gaps by averaging neighbours by default (or treats them as zero), auto-detects seasonality (maximum season length 8,760), and averages duplicate timestamps. — [Microsoft FORECAST.ETS reference](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/excel/forecast-ets-function)

*C. Pricing and price-elasticity what-ifs*
- Data: units sold, realised net price after discounts and rebates, invoice date, product and channel IDs, promotion details, and competitor prices if available. Controls for trend, seasonality and major marketing pushes are required. — [MCP Analytics elasticity guide](https://mcpanalytics.ai/articles/price-elasticity-practical-guide-for-data-driven-decisions); [Umbrex elasticity guide](https://umbrex.com/resources/company-analysis/product-management/product-price-elasticity-estimate/)
- One source recommends at least 3–6 months of data containing several price changes, with prices varying about 10–15%. Estimates from two price points or a few weeks of data are unreliable. — [Symson: data quality for elasticity](https://www.symson.com/blog/what-kind-of-data-quality-do-you-need-to-run-price-elasticity-analysis-for-your-products); [MCP Analytics](https://mcpanalytics.ai/articles/price-elasticity-practical-guide-for-data-driven-decisions)
- Sparsity: with k distinct recent price points an item yields only k observations, and k is often under 5. — [USPTO patent 11429992](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11429992)
- When history lacks price variation, the fallback is A/B or switchback tests (e.g. 10% of new signups see a different price for 4 weeks). — [Speero](https://speero.com/post/price-elasticity-101-how-to-test-prices-without-wrecking-your-data-or-your-customers); [Stripe price testing](https://stripe.com/resources/more/price-testing)
- Break-even volume rule for a price cut: required volume lift = price cut % ÷ (margin % − price cut %). For example, a 5% cut at a 30% margin needs about a 20% volume lift. — [MCP Analytics](https://mcpanalytics.ai/articles/price-elasticity-practical-guide-for-data-driven-decisions)

*D. Break-even and unit economics*
- Formulas: break-even units = fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost per unit); break-even revenue = fixed costs ÷ contribution-margin ratio. The analysis is only valid within the "relevant range", and step and semi-variable costs break the simple model. — [LibreTexts small business accounting](https://biz.libretexts.org/Courses/Reedley_College/Operation_of_a_Small_Business_for_College_Students/10%3A_Accounting_for_Small_Business/10.02%3A_Revenue_Cost_and_Break-Even_Analysis); [Beancount guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/05/15/break-even-analysis-contribution-margin-formula-fixed-variable-costs-margin-of-safety-small-business-pricing-decisions-guide)
- The SBA break-even calculator works on monthly fixed costs, variable costs and unit sales, and is framed as an estimate for business plans and lenders. — [SBA break-even calculator](https://sba.gov/es/node/56191)

*E. Marketing budget allocation, ROAS/CAC, MMM*
- Google Meridian guidance (secondary): about 2 years of weekly data for geo-level models and 3 years for national models. Monthly-only data needs at least 3 years and can be unstable. — [Appier Meridian guide](https://www.appier.com/en/blog/what-is-marketing-mix-modeling-mmm-a-complete-guide-to-meridian-and-how-it-revolutionizes-traditional-approaches); [Dolphin Analytics](https://www.dolphinanalytics.co.uk/blog/marketing-mix-modelling-meridian)
- Meta Robyn guidance (secondary): about 2 years of weekly data or 4–5 years of monthly data, with about 10 observations per independent variable. — [EGGKNITE Meridian vs Robyn](https://www.eggknite.com/blog/google-meridian-vs-meta-robyn)
- Lower practitioner thresholds (52+ weeks, or 18+ months weekly) also circulate. Channel grouping (5–7 channels) and incrementality tests are recommended when data is thin. — [AdLibrary MMM playbook](https://adlibrary.com/posts/mmm); [MatrixROI MMM for businesses without a data team](https://matrixroi.com/resources/marketing-mix-modeling-basics)
- Data: weekly spend by channel, weekly revenue or conversions, promotions, prices, seasonality and holiday flags, and ideally impressions or clicks.

*F. Churn and customer lifetime value*
- BG/NBD plus Gamma-Gamma needs one row per order with customer ID, transaction date and order value. These are summarised per customer into frequency, recency, observation length T and average order value. Output is P(alive) and expected future transactions and value. The model is for non-contractual repeat purchase; subscriptions with explicit cancellation should use survival or cohort retention curves. Multi-line exports such as Shopify's need deduplication to one row per order. The origin is Fader, Hardie & Lee (2005). — [MetricGate BG/NBD docs](https://metricgate.com/docs/clv-bg-nbd/); [MCP Analytics BG/NBD](https://mcpanalytics.ai/articles/analytics__customer__ltv__bg_nbd); [ClicData CLV guide](https://www.clicdata.com/blog/customer-lifetime-value-prediction/)

*G. Staffing and workforce planning (restaurant and retail example)*
- Forecast sales at the smallest interval that changes staffing: 15 minutes for quick-service, dayparts for full-service. Use POS history, for example the average of the last 8 weeks excluding outliers and adjusted for holidays and events, plus year-over-year monthly comparisons. Convert the result to labour hours using a target labour percentage, and compare scheduled with actual labour, ideally polled every 15, 30 or 60 minutes from the POS. — [Simplot labor forecasting](https://simplotfood.com/blog/how-to-forecast-your-restaurant-sales-to-help-with-scheduling); [Restaurant365 intraday polling](https://www.restaurant365.com/blog/how-to-optimize-labor-costs-same-day-polling/); [ADP restaurant labor](https://www.adp.com/spark/articles/2017/07/maximizing-time-and-labor-in-restaurant-management.aspx)

*H. Scenario planning (driver-based)*
- FP&A Trends 2025 (secondary summaries): driver-based models correlate strongly with self-rated forecast accuracy (77% versus 27%) and with forecast speed (20% of driver-based teams forecast in under 2 days). Only 18% can run scenarios in under a day, and 39% cite lack of dynamic tools as the top barrier. — [FP&A Trends 2025 benchmarks](https://fpa-trends.com/article/2025-fpa-benchmarks-and-trends); [The Finance Weekly roundup](https://www.thefinanceweekly.com/post/all-of-the-top-fpa-surveys-and-stats-of-2025)

### Inferences
Summary table of minimum data, common SMB data problems and fit-for-purpose methods. Synthesised from the sources above; the specific month thresholds are rules of thumb consistent with Hyndman's parameter-counting argument, not hard rules.

| Type | Minimum practical data | Common SMB data problems | Fit-for-purpose methods |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13-week cash flow | Current bank balances; open AR and AP aging with due dates; payroll calendar; recurring bills, loans and tax dates; 6–12 months of bank transactions to learn collection lags | Unreconciled books; AR not aged or credit notes missing; owner draws mixed with business; cash-basis books; uncategorised bank lines | Rules-based schedule plus collection-lag estimates; ETS or seasonal naive only for the "other receipts" line; scenario toggles |
| Budget / P&L projection | 12–24 months of monthly P&L by account; headcount list with salaries; known contracts | Inconsistent chart of accounts; accrual vs cash mixing; one-off items not tagged | Driver-based model (volume × price, headcount × cost), prior year plus growth, rolling forecast |
| Sales / revenue | 24–36 months of monthly (or 2+ years weekly) sales by product, channel or customer, so ETS can see at least 2 seasonal cycles | Product codes changing; returns netted inconsistently; promotions and price changes not logged; COVID-era breaks | Seasonal naive, ETS (FORECAST.ETS), regression with drivers, combinations; pooled gradient boosting only with many SKUs and stores |
| Demand / inventory | Transaction-level POS or e-commerce lines (date, SKU, qty, price, store), stock-on-hand, lead times, MOQ | Stockouts hide true demand (censored sales); intermittent SKUs; no lead-time records | Croston / intermittent methods, ETS by SKU, reorder point = lead-time demand + safety stock; hierarchical or pooled ML at scale |
| Pricing what-if | Margin by product (price and unit cost); for elasticity, sales at 3+ distinct price points with promo flags | Little or no price variation; discounts not recorded at line level | Break-even volume arithmetic; log-log regression where variation exists; switchback/A-B tests |
| Break-even / unit economics | Fixed-cost list; variable cost per unit or order; price; for CAC payback, spend and new customers by month | Costs not split fixed vs variable; shared costs unallocated | Closed-form formulas plus sensitivity tables |
| Marketing ROAS/CAC/MMM | CAC/ROAS: monthly spend by channel plus new customers and revenue. MMM: about 2–3 years of weekly spend by channel, revenue, promos and seasonality | Platform-reported conversions double-count; offline sales unattributed; too little spend variation | Simple CAC/ROAS trends and payback; geo or holdout tests; Bayesian MMM (Meridian/Robyn) only with enough history |
| Churn / CLV | 12+ months of order-level history with stable customer IDs; for subscriptions, start and cancel dates | Guest checkouts, no customer ID; duplicate customers; multi-line exports | Cohort retention tables; BG/NBD + Gamma-Gamma; survival curves for subscriptions |
| Staffing / labour | 8+ weeks (ideally 1+ year) of POS sales by 15-minute or daypart interval; timesheets; labour standards | POS and time clock not linked; seasonality short | Weekly-seasonal averages or ETS at interval level; labour-% or units-per-labour-hour conversion |
| Scenario planning | A working driver-based P&L and cash model (above) | Absent baseline model | Driver toggles, sensitivity and tornado analysis, simple Monte Carlo on key drivers |

- The competition evidence (M4: pure ML underperformed and statistical combinations and hybrids won; M5: LightGBM won on large, hierarchical retail data) suggests that a tool for SMBs with short, noisy series should default to robust statistical baselines and combinations (seasonal naive, ETS, simple regression). ML should be reserved for clients with many SKUs or locations. This favours a product built on transparent methods plus LLM-written narrative, not heavy ML.
- Data preparation will dominate the work: reconciling books, mapping the chart of accounts, and tagging one-offs. FP&A teams with dedicated staff still spend about 45–46% of their time on data collection and validation (section 3), and SMB data is usually messier.

### Gaps
- Microsoft describes FORECAST.ETS as using the "AAA version" of exponential triple smoothing (additive error, trend, season). This could not be re-verified in this session because the Microsoft page was unreachable.
- No authoritative SMB-specific benchmark was found for minimum history length per method. The thresholds in the table are inferred.
- No independent (non-vendor) data was found on SMB inventory forecast accuracy or stockout rates.

## 3. Current practice and pain: spreadsheets, time, accuracy, and consequences of bad forecasts

### Takeaway
Spreadsheets dominate even among professional FP&A teams: AFP 2025 found 96% use them for planning. FP&A teams spend nearly half their time collecting and validating data, and most take more than 5 days to produce a forecast. For SMBs without finance staff, the evidence points to owners, bookkeepers or outside accountants, with many firms not forecasting at all. The popular "82% of businesses fail because of cash flow" statistic cannot be traced to a primary source and should not be cited as fact.

### Cited Findings
**Spreadsheet reliance and tooling (mostly mid-market and enterprise FP&A, not micro-SMBs)**

### Inferences
- For SMBs below about 50 employees, the realistic "current practice" is an annual budget in Excel built by the owner or an outside accountant, ad-hoc cash checks of the bank balance, and gut feel for pricing and hiring. This is inferred from the absence of in-house FP&A, the UK data on many SMEs never forecasting, and accountants' growing but still developing advisory practices.
- Price anchors: a one-off AI-generated report at a few hundred dollars, or a subscription of $50–$300 a month, would sit between DIY software ($20–$50 a month) and fractional CFOs ($2,500+ a month). This is a pricing inference, not tested demand.
- Egypt and MENA: longer collection cycles (66–81 days, unverified) and invoice-tracking pain (30% of UAE SMEs) make AR-driven cash forecasting more valuable there. However, informality, manual books and cash-basis records mean more data cleaning per client. Saudi and UAE e-invoicing mandates may make structured invoice data more available than in Egypt.

- The primary FP&A Trends 2025 and AFP 2025 documents could not be opened. Figures come from search summaries.
- No Egypt-specific survey was found on SMB forecasting or budgeting practice. MSMEDA and CAPMAS publications were not reachable.
- The 82% statistic remains unverified at source.

## 4. Which forecasts produce decision-ready outputs an owner would pay for

### Takeaway
The most "payable" outputs are those that convert directly into a dated action with a money amount. Examples: "cash falls below payroll in week 9; chase these 3 invoices or delay this $X payment", "a 10% price rise breaks even unless volume falls by more than 25%", "you can afford one hire in March if collections hold". These come from the 13-week cash flow, pricing and break-even what-ifs, and driver-based scenarios, which are also the analyses with the lowest data requirements.

### Cited Findings
- Practitioner 13-week guidance explicitly separates discretionary, movable outflows such as marketing, travel and contractors, and recommends weekly variance review. This structure lends itself to "what to cut or delay" recommendations. — [Control-Fi](https://control-fi.com/blog/13-week-cash-flow-forecast/); [Centime](https://www.centime.com/articles/13-week-cash-flow-forecast)
- Ledger-native forecasts in Xero and QuickBooks project from invoice due dates and bank feeds but don't answer scenario questions such as hiring or losing a major client. — [Finoya](https://www.finoya.ai/blog/ledger-ai-cash-flow-limits/); [CXCash](https://cxcash.com/blog/best-cash-flow-forecasting-software-small-business)
- Scenario speed is a recognised gap even for professional teams: only 18% can run scenarios in under a day, and 39% blame a lack of dynamic tools. — [FP&A Trends 2025 benchmarks](https://fpa-trends.com/article/2025-fpa-benchmarks-and-trends)
- Price-change what-ifs reduce to a clear break-even volume threshold (price cut % ÷ (margin − cut %)). Sources recommend comparing profit, not revenue, across +5%, +10% and +20% scenarios at different elasticities. — [MCP Analytics](https://mcpanalytics.ai/articles/price-elasticity-practical-guide-for-data-driven-decisions)
- SBA frames break-even as a key number for lenders and business plans. — [SBA break-even calculator](https://sba.gov/es/node/56191)
- Accountants report clients increasingly expect forward-looking advice (35% name it a top challenge), and 79% expect advisory to grow. This suggests accountants as a channel or buyer for white-label reports. — [Xero US 2025](https://www.xero.com/us/media-releases/us-state-of-the-industry-report-2025/); [Intuit 2025 Accountant Tech](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/small-business-data/accountant-tech-survey-2025/)

### Inferences
- Ranking by "decision-readiness × data availability × pain":
  1. 13-week cash forecast with runway date and ranked levers (collect, delay, cut, borrow)
  2. Price and cost pass-through what-if with break-even volume table
  3. Hire or expansion affordability scenario (new hire, new branch, new van) layered on a driver-based P&L and cash model
  4. Break-even and unit-economics card per product or location
  5. Inventory reorder and cash-tied-in-stock report (product businesses)
  6. Staffing schedule recommendations (restaurants and retail)
  7. CAC/ROAS and payback report (digital-heavy SMBs)
  8. CLV/churn segmentation (repeat-purchase SMBs)
- Price arithmetic for a +10% price rise at a 30% gross margin: break-even volume loss = 10/(30+10) = 25%. This is arithmetic from the same formula family, not a cited benchmark. Outputs like this are easy to explain in plain language, a strength for LLM-written reports.
- Outputs owners are less likely to pay for: generic multi-year revenue projections without actions, and MMM or ML outputs whose data requirements the business can't meet (they would be unreliable and hard to trust).
- Risk: the cash forecast is only as good as the AR/AP data and the reconciliation behind it, so an AI report product needs a data-readiness check, such as "books reconciled through X date", and should show uncertainty bands rather than false precision. The guidance to show uncertain receipts separately supports this.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence (survey or willingness-to-pay study) was found on which specific report outputs SMB owners would pay for, or at what price.
- No data was found on how often SMB owners act on forecasts, or on the measured ROI of cash forecasting for small firms.

## 5. How courses and certifications teach forecasting vs current practice

### Takeaway
Mainstream business-analytics courses teach classical Excel-based time-series methods: naive, moving averages, exponential smoothing (Holt and Winters), decomposition, regression with trend and seasonality, and sometimes ARIMA. This matches the robust statistical baselines that won or stayed competitive in the M-competitions for short series. What courses usually under-teach is SMB finance work: direct-method cash-flow schedules from AR/AP, driver-based 3-statement modelling, scenario design, intermittent demand, causal pricing and marketing measurement, and data cleaning of real ledgers.

### Cited Findings
- Coursera / Macquarie University "Excel Skills for Business Forecasting" (3 courses): Course 1 covers time-series models (naïve, moving averages, trend fitting, simple exponential smoothing, Holt, Winters, decomposition), Course 2 regression models, and Course 3 judgmental forecasting. — [Coursera Macquarie](https://www-cloudfront-alias.coursera.org/learn/excel-business-forecasting-time-series)
- Statistics.com "Forecasting Analytics" (Galit Shmueli) covers regression models, smoothing methods (moving average and exponential smoothing) and autoregressive models. The Class Central listing shows a progression from moving averages and smoothing through regression trend and seasonality to AR and ARIMA. — [Statistics.com](https://www.statistics.com/courses/forecasting-analytics/); [Class Central](https://classcentral.com/course/business-analytics-forecasting-6739)
- Wilmington University MBA 7720 emphasises Excel for building forecasting models (regression, moving average, exponential smoothing). — [Wilmington MBA 7720](https://wilmu.smartcatalogiq.com/en/sandbox/graduate-catalog/courses/mba-business-administration/7000/mba-7720)
- Liberty University BUSI 405 "Forecasting and Predictive Analytics" exists as an undergraduate offering. Its syllabus details were not retrieved. — [Liberty BUSI 405](https://www.liberty.edu/online/courses/BUSI405/)
- Excel's built-in FORECAST.ETS and Forecast Sheet automate exponential smoothing with automatic seasonality detection and missing-data handling (up to 30%). — [Microsoft FORECAST.ETS](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/excel/forecast-ets-function)
- Current practice in professional FP&A emphasises driver-based models (77% good accuracy against 27% for basic models), rolling forecasts and scenario speed, not more sophisticated time-series algorithms. — [FP&A Trends 2025 benchmarks](https://fpa-trends.com/article/2025-fpa-benchmarks-and-trends)
- Research frontier: M4 favoured statistical combinations and hybrids over pure ML, while M5 (large hierarchical retail data) favoured LightGBM. — [M4 press release](https://forecasters.org/blog/2018/06/19/the-m-competitions-and-their-far-reaching-contributions-to-the-theory-and-practice-of-forecasting); [M5 paper](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/M5_accuracy_competition.pdf)

### Inferences
- A founder trained on Excel forecasting (FORECAST.ETS, regression, moving averages) already has the right statistical core for SMB-scale series. The gaps to close are mostly finance mechanics: the 13-week direct cash method, accrual vs cash timing, the 3-statement linkage, and driver-based budgeting. Productisation also needs data wrangling of QuickBooks/Xero/POS exports, plus communicating uncertainty, for example ETS prediction intervals.
- AI/LLM value-add is less about a better point forecast (the M4 evidence cautions against assuming ML wins on short series) and more about three things: automating data cleaning and categorisation, producing scenario variants quickly, and translating outputs into plain-language actions.

### Gaps
- Curricula for CFI's FMVA, IIBA business-analysis certifications and Google's data-analytics certificate were not retrieved, so their forecasting coverage is unconfirmed.
- No source was found comparing what business-analysis graduates learn with what SMB advisory work requires.
