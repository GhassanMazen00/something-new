# Technical feasibility, building blocks and accuracy limits of an AI business-forecasting and what-if system (solo beginner + Claude)

Research date: 2026-10-26. Method note for the report writer: direct page fetching was blocked in this session (arXiv, NeurIPS, GitHub API and several blogs returned DNS errors or proxy 403s), so most figures below come from search-engine excerpts of the named primary sources, not from my own reading of full papers. Each figure is attributed to the page the excerpt came from. Numbers that matter to a decision should be re-checked against the primary page before publication. Claude API facts come from Anthropic's bundled API reference (cached 2026-10-06) and are cited to the official docs pages that reference points to.

## 1. Time-series forecasting options in 2026 and their measured accuracy (incl. short, noisy SMB series, licences, cost)

### Takeaway
In 2026, pretrained "foundation" forecasters (Chronos-2, TimesFM-2.5/3, TiRex, Moirai 2, and newer claimants such as Mistral's Timestral) top the big public leaderboards (GIFT-Eval, fev-bench). For the short monthly series typical of SMBs (24–36 points), though, the evidence is mixed. Independent studies find well-tuned statistical ensembles (ETS/ARIMA/Theta) match or beat foundation models on many short, low-frequency datasets. The realistic design is to run both and let a rolling backtest choose per series. Licensing matters a lot: Chronos (Apache-2.0) and TimesFM-2.5 (Apache-2.0) can be used commercially, while TimesFM-3 and Moirai-2.0 weights are non-commercial.

### Cited Findings

**Leaderboards (big, mixed-domain benchmarks)**
- In the Chronos-2 paper's GIFT-Eval table (Oct 2025), Chronos-2 leads on probabilistic accuracy (WQL) with a 79.8% win rate and 46.6% skill score. Next are TiRex (70.4% / 41.7%), TimesFM-2.5 (70.0% / 42.4%) and Moirai-2.0 (56.0% / 40.9%). On MASE: Chronos-2 81.5% / 26.5%, TimesFM-2.5 71.6%, TiRex 67.1%. The baseline numbers are taken from the GIFT-Eval leaderboard. — [Chronos-2 paper, arXiv 2510.15821](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.15821); [Amazon Science blog](https://www.amazon.science/blog/introducing-chronos-2-from-univariate-to-universal-forecasting)
- fev-bench has 100 forecasting tasks across seven domains, 46 of them with covariates. It reports win rates and skill scores with bootstrapped confidence intervals. In the paper, Chronos-2 has the highest win rate and skill score, followed by TiRex and TimesFM-2.5, and the authors call Chronos-2's lead statistically significant (v2 revised 3 Feb 2026). — [fev-bench, arXiv 2509.26468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26468v2)
- One of the fev-bench authors says that on tasks with covariates, top models gain up to about 10 skill-score points when they can see the exogenous factors (post undated). — [Abdul Fatir Ansari, LinkedIn](https://de.linkedin.com/in/abdulfatir)
- Independent "living" benchmark LiveHouse-TS: Chronos-2 narrowly leads overall (0.671) ahead of TiRex (0.668), with TimesFM-2.5 at 0.609. TimesFM-2.5 leads the MSE point-forecast track (0.680), and Chronos-2 ranks first under CRPS. — [LiveHouse-TS, arXiv 2608.17299](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17299)
- Moirai 2.0: Salesforce's launch post says it ranks #1 by MASE on GIFT-Eval "among all non test data leaking models". A third-party article says the paper itself places it 5th on MASE and 6th on CRPS. The two claims conflict. — [Salesforce blog](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/moirai-2-0/); [beri.net commentary](https://www.beri.net/article/timesfm-vs-chronos-vs-moirai-time-series-foundation-model-licence-bigquery-sagemaker-cost-2026); [Moirai 2.0 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.11698)
- **Possibly outdated / unverified newer claims:** Google's TimesFM-3 (reported launch 31 Aug 2026, 330M parameters, native multivariate and covariate support) is said by a secondary blog to rank #1 among foundation models on GIFT-Eval. One source says the benchmark evidence so far comes from Google's launch material, not from a full paper. — [MachineLearningMastery 2026 toolkit](https://machinelearningmastery.com/the-2026-time-series-toolkit-5-foundation-models-for-autonomous-forecasting/); [TSFM.ai](https://tsfm.ai/blog/timesfm-3-multivariate-zero-shot-forecasting); [explainx.ai](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/google-timesfm-3-multivariate-time-series-foundation-model-2026)
- **Unverified:** a news item dated 2026-10-19 reports Mistral's claim that its 209M-parameter "Timestral" is #1 on GIFT-Eval, TIME and fev-bench. This is the vendor's own claim and I could not check it against the leaderboards. — [StartupHub.ai](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/ai-research/2026/mistral-says-209m-timestral-leads-on-three-benchmarks.md)

**Independent evidence that classical methods remain competitive**
- A NeurIPS 2025 paper ("A More Realistic Evaluation of Cross-Frequency Transfer Learning and Foundation Forecasting Models") finds that statistical models and their ensembles consistently outperform existing foundation forecasting models, by more than 8.2% in sCRPS and more than 20% in MASE across datasets. It also argues that statistical models' accuracy is frequently underreported. — [NeurIPS 2025 poster page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/130449)
- On M3 Monthly in the VisionTS paper's table, Theta (623.71) beats MOIRAI-Small (713.41). On Tourism Monthly, Theta (2069.96) and ETS (2004.51) beat MOIRAI-Small (3569.85) and LLMTime (5608.61). The excerpt does not name the metric. — [VisionTS, arXiv 2408.17253](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.17253)
- An older blog analysis found Chronos best on M3 monthly but behind Theta on yearly and quarterly series. Theta scored about 0.87–0.88 median MASE at every frequency (the analysis is about 800 days old). — [Medium: Outsmarting Time](https://medium.com/@kristian.ertresvaag/outsmarting-time-foundation-models-for-zero-shot-forecasting-54e66619b89d)
- A July 2026 break-even analysis notes that for very short datasets (M3-Monthly, M3-Quarterly, Hospital, Dominick, M4-Hourly, ILI, all with n_train < 100), the smallest training fractions leave fewer than 50 samples. That is below what reliable classical fitting needs, particularly for ARIMA. In that setup foundation models get the full history as context. Tourism-Monthly is one case where Chronos-Bolt-B beats the best classical method (ETS). — [When Do Foundation Models Pay Off?, arXiv 2607.04919](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04919v1)
- M5 (Walmart, 42,840 series): the LightGBM winner was about 3% more accurate than the organisers' exponential-smoothing benchmark. A later replication found ES slightly more accurate than the LightGBM ensemble (by 0.57%) and attributed the gap to implementation details. A newsletter's "22% better" claim could not be verified. — [Wielding Occam's razor, arXiv 2102.13209](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.13209); [M5 results paper record](https://pure.unic.ac.cy/en/publications/m5-accuracy-competition-results-findings-and-conclusions/); [Conscious Supply Chain newsletter](https://conscious-supply-chain.beehiiv.com/p/forecasting-learning-from-competitions-issue-06-3230)
- Hyndman and Athanasopoulos: textbook minimum-sample rules (such as "30 for ARIMA") are "misleading and unsubstantiated". The only hard limit is more observations than parameters, but in practice substantially more are needed, and how many depends on noise. Hyndman & Kostenko (2007) conclude there are no simple sample-size rules. — [FPP excerpt via KNIME forum](https://forum.knime.com/t/knime-decompose-signal/36511); [Hyndman & Kostenko 2007, Foresight](https://ideas.repec.org/a/for/ijafaa/y2007i6p12-15.html)
- Nixtla StatsForecast fits AutoARIMA, AutoETS, AutoCES and AutoTheta and selects the best model per series by cross-validation. — [Nixtla blog: statsforecast automatic model selection](https://www.nixtla.io/blog/statsforecast-automatic-model-selection)

The "250x faster" Bolt claim compares Bolt with the original Chronos, not with Chronos-2. — [CITRAS-FM, arXiv 2606.10798](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.10798); [Darts Chronos-2 docs](https://unit8co.github.io/darts/generated_api/darts.models.forecasting.chronos2_model.html); [Towards Data Science](https://towardsdatascience.com/five-questions-about-chronos-2-the-time-series-foundation-model/)

TimesFM-3 (330M) code is Apache-2.0, but its weights carry a non-commercial licence, so default 3.0 weights cannot be used commercially or in production. Release dates and context lengths conflict across sources (about 15,360 vs 16,384). — [letsdatascience](https://letsdatascience.com/news/timesfm-releases-25-time-series-model-update-416fba8f); [pasqualepillitteri.it comparison](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/26634/time-series-ai-compared-timesfm-chronos-timestral); [beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/timesfm-vs-chronos-vs-moirai-time-series-foundation-model-licence-bigquery-sagemaker-cost-2026); [MarkTechPost on 2.5](https://www.marktechpost.com/2025/09/16/google-ai-ships-timesfm-2-5-smaller-longer-context-foundation-model-that-now-leads-gift-eval-zero-shot-forecasting/)

The uni2ts library is MIT according to its README. — [HF Moirai-2.0-R-small](https://huggingface.co/Salesforce/moirai-2.0-R-small); [uni2ts](https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/uni2ts)
- Nixtla TimeGPT is a hosted API. Starter is free (50 calls/month), Standard $99/month (10,000 calls), Premium $399/month (100,000 calls), Enterprise custom, with self-hosting on request. Azure pay-as-you-go lists about $0.00013 per 1K input tokens and $0.00038 per 1K output tokens. - Nixtla's TimeGPT-1 paper claims it beats statistical, ML and deep-learning baselines zero-shot. This is a vendor claim. — [summary via search; TimeGPT paper alphaxiv](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2310.03589v3)

### Inferences
- For an SMB with 24–36 monthly points, the honest default is a small ensemble: SeasonalNaive, AutoETS, AutoTheta and (where enough data) AutoARIMA via statsforecast, plus Chronos-Bolt-small or Chronos-2 run zero-shot on CPU. Pick or weight models by a rolling-origin backtest on that company's own history. Choosing a model by leaderboard rank is not justified, because leaderboard winners differ by metric, and independent work shows classical ensembles often win on short, low-frequency data.
- With 24–36 monthly points, at most 2–3 full seasonal cycles exist. That leaves only a handful of backtest windows (for example 6–12 one-step-ahead origins), so the accuracy estimate is itself noisy. The product should present accuracy as a range, not a single number.
- Commercially safe foundation-model choices as of Oct 2026: the Chronos family (Apache-2.0) and TimesFM-2.5 (Apache-2.0). Avoid TimesFM-3 default weights and Moirai-2.0 in a commercial product unless licences change. Timestral's licence is unknown (gap).
- At SMB scale (tens to hundreds of series, short context), CPU inference with Chronos-Bolt or Chronos-2 should take seconds, so no GPU is needed. The 1.79 s per window figure for Chronos-2 used a 1,032-step history, far longer than 36 points. This is an extrapolation, not measured.
- TimeGPT's free tier (50 calls/month) is enough for prototyping. Self-hosted open models remove both per-call cost and the need to send customer data to a third party.

### Gaps
- I could not open the live GIFT-Eval or fev-bench leaderboards, so current (Oct 2026) rankings, including where TimesFM-3, Timestral, TiRex-2 and Toto sit, are unverified.
- No sourced measurements of IBM Granite TTM (r2) licence and accuracy, or of Lag-Llama accuracy, came back from searches. (Lag-Llama is mentioned only as a baseline in the break-even paper.)
- No benchmark specifically on SMB-style series of 24–36 monthly points with structural breaks was found. Evidence for that regime is extrapolated from M3/Tourism and the break-even paper.
- No sourced data on Chronos quantile calibration (empirical coverage of its prediction intervals) on short series.

## 2. What-if / causal / scenario tools: what can be estimated credibly from SMB-sized data?

### Takeaway
Driver-based models with Monte Carlo ranges are fully feasible for SMBs because they need assumptions, not big data. Statistically estimated causal effects mostly are not. Marketing-mix models (Meridian, Robyn, PyMC-Marketing) officially need about 2–3 years of weekly data and several channels. Double-ML causal estimators need hundreds of observations. A typical SMB with 24–36 monthly rows can credibly get scenario arithmetic, rough elasticity ranges with wide error bars, and before/after comparisons. It cannot get reliable channel ROI or causal attributions.

### Cited Findings
- Google Meridian docs: historical data should generally cover at least two years of weekly data for geo-level models and three years for national-level models. With only monthly data, the docs recommend at least three years. Gaps must be imputed or zero-filled. — [Meridian: collect and organize data](https://developers.google.com/meridian/docs/pre-modeling/collect-data)
- Google's own Meridian example (2 years weekly, 12 media channels, 6 controls) yields only about 4 data points per estimated effect, which Google calls a low-sample-size scenario. — [Meridian: collect and organize data](https://developers.google.com/meridian/docs/pre-modeling/collect-data)
- A vendor blog claims Meridian can work with 12–18 months plus experiments or strong priors, which contradicts the official guidance. — [stellaheystella blog](https://www.stellaheystella.com/blog/getting-started-with-mmm-using-google-meridian)
- Robyn academic paper: at least two years of weekly observations recommended, and a "one in ten" variables-to-observations rule of thumb. — [Packaging Up Media Mix Modeling, arXiv 2403.14674](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.14674)
- MSI 2026 working paper: PyMC-Marketing is fully Bayesian and highly customisable, aimed at advanced practitioners, and needs Bayesian expertise around priors, plus customisation for budget optimisation. Robyn is the most automated option and supports budget optimisation out of the box, but its first full run can take up to two weeks. — [MSI Report 26-106](https://thearf-org-unified-admin.s3.amazonaws.com/MSI/2026/MSI_Report_26-106.pdf)
- Robyn is described by Meta as "experimental" and "semi-automated", and its Python option (beta) still requires the R package. — [spaceads.agency 2026](https://www.spaceads.agency/blog/marketing-mix-modeling-meridian-robyn-mmm-2026)
- Practitioner guidance: if the monthly ad budget is small, one channel dominates spend, or there is less than a year of history, MMM will not give reliable results. A well-instrumented holdout test gives cleaner answers more cheaply (vendor/practitioner blog). — [thegrowthfolks: MMM on a small budget](https://thegrowthfolks.com/blog/marketing-mix-modelling-small-budgets/)
- Meridian GeoX FAQ: geo experiments should use at least 10 geos for a single-cell design, and power is low with few geos. — [Meridian GeoX FAQs](https://developers.google.com/meridian/geox/faqs)
- Double/debiased ML (the method family behind EconML/DoubleML) relies on Neyman-orthogonal scores, cross-fitting and sample splitting. Its guarantees are asymptotic (√N-consistency). — [Chernozhukov et al., arXiv 1608.00060](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.00060)
- A panel-data study finds DML methods are not guaranteed to improve much with sample size, and gains depend on the number of observed confounders. — [DML meets panel data, arXiv 2409.01266](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.01266)
- Low-authority rule of thumb: cross-fitting is unreliable with n < 200 (unverified calculator site). — [metricgate DML docs](https://metricgate.com/docs/double-machine-learning)
- For volatile, limited data, a zero-inflated negative binomial mixed-effects model (site, time and weather effects) predicted foodservice sales by category "accurately with limited data" during geopolitical and economic disruption (Journal of Applied Statistics, 2025). Detailed error metrics were not retrieved. — [Judd et al. 2025, PMC12872089](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12872089/)

### Inferences
- The what-if engine a beginner can build credibly is a **driver tree** (revenue = traffic × conversion × average price; cost = COGS% × revenue + fixed costs, with FX pass-through for imported inputs), plus **Monte Carlo** sampling (numpy) over user-supplied or backtest-derived ranges, plus the statistical baseline forecast. This is arithmetic in code, and its credibility comes from transparent assumptions, not from estimation.
- Price elasticity: with only a few dozen monthly rows and few price changes, a log-log regression gives an elasticity estimate with wide confidence intervals. It should be shown as a range and flagged when the CI includes zero. Transaction-level POS data (thousands of rows across SKUs and branches) makes this much more feasible than monthly aggregates. This is inferred from DML and MMM sample-size guidance; no SMB-specific elasticity source was found.
- MMM, DoWhy and EconML should be out of scope for an MVP aimed at typical SMBs, or offered only to the few customers with 2+ years of weekly multi-channel spend. Holdout or geo tests and simple before/after comparisons are the honest alternative.

### Gaps
- No source quantified the minimum data needed for DoWhy or EconML specifically, or gave SMB price-elasticity case studies.
- PyMC Labs' reported "6 months of weekly data" MMM claim could not be verified against a primary source.
- Robyn's maintenance status in 2026 is unconfirmed.

## 3. LLMs in the loop: what to delegate, where they must not be trusted, best practice

### Takeaway
Use the LLM as orchestrator and explainer. It can map messy columns, write and run pandas code, narrate results and draft recommendations. It should not produce forecasts or do arithmetic in its head. Peer-reviewed evidence shows LLM-based forecasters add little or nothing over simple models, and LLM-only arithmetic and table-filling are error-prone. Claude's code-execution tool can run the data cleaning, but forecasting libraries such as statsforecast and Chronos are not pre-installed and the sandbox has no internet, so heavy forecasting belongs in the app's own Python backend.

### Cited Findings
- Tan et al. (NeurIPS 2024): removing the LLM from three popular LLM-based forecasters, or replacing it with a simple attention layer, did not hurt accuracy and often improved it. Pretrained LLMs did no better than models trained from scratch and gave no benefit in few-shot settings, despite high compute cost. — [NeurIPS 2024 proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6ed5bf446f59e2c6646d23058c86424b-Abstract.html); [arXiv 2406.16964](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16964v1)
- Cao & Wang (2024): on deterministic metrics like MAE, LLMs perform worse than conventional techniques like ARIMA, and LLMTIME degrades as values grow over time. They tested gpt-3.5-turbo-instruct, not GPT-4. — [arXiv 2408.04867](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.04867)
- The original LLMTIME paper reports favourable zero-shot CRPS and likelihood versus ARIMA, so the evidence is mixed by metric. — [LLMTIME, arXiv 2310.07820](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.07820)
- An empirical study of LLM reasoning traces lists arithmetic miscalculation as a recognised failure category, where an early error propagates through later steps. — [arXiv 2512.00215](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.00215)
- In data-science notebook code generation, the main execution errors are KeyError and AttributeError from referencing non-existent DataFrame columns or indexes. Executing code catches this class of error, but not wrong-but-runnable logic. — [arXiv 2212.09248](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.09248)
- Spreadsheet error-finding (FLARE benchmark, ResearchGate listing): Gemini 2.5 Pro found 12/12 seeded errors, while ChatGPT-4.5 and o3-mini-high found 11/12. The benchmark is small and not peer-reviewed. — [FLARE, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392942915_Large_Language_Models_for_Spreadsheets_Benchmarking_Progress_and_Evaluating_Performance_with_FLARE)
- A hobbyist benchmark of LLMs filling a spreadsheet-like table by internal reasoning alone scored from 100% down to 0% depending on the model (methodology unverified). — [llm-arithmetic-benchmark](https://github.com/hashtagchris/llm-arithmetic-benchmark)
- Claude code-execution tool: isolated container (1 CPU, 5 GiB RAM, 5 GiB disk), no internet access, Python 3.11. Pre-installed: pandas, numpy, scipy, scikit-learn, statsmodels, matplotlib, seaborn, openpyxl, xlsxwriter, pdfplumber, python-docx and python-pptx. Accepts CSV, Excel (.xlsx/.xls), JSON and XML. Containers persist 30 days and can be reused. It is free with web search/fetch, otherwise $0.05/hour after 1,550 free hours per month per organisation. (The reference also says extra packages can be pip-installed at runtime, which conflicts with "no internet"; verify.) — [Anthropic docs: code execution tool](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/code-execution-tool)
- Claude API prices (first-party, per million tokens, as of the 2026-10-06 cache): Claude Opus 5.5 $4 input / $20 output, Claude Sonnet 5.5 $2 / $10, Claude Haiku 5.5 $0.10 / $0.50 (prompts up to 100K tokens; $0.50 / $2.50 beyond). Batch API is 50% off. Prompt-cache reads cost about 0.1× base input (0.05× on Opus 5.5) and cache writes 1.25× (5-minute TTL). — [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [Anthropic models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview); [Prompt caching docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)
- The Claude API supports structured outputs (`output_config.format`, JSON-schema-constrained responses) and strict tool use (`strict: true` guarantees tool inputs validate against the schema). This suits column-mapping output that code then checks. — [Anthropic models overview / tool-use docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview)

### Inferences
- Safe division of labour:
  - **LLM does:** map columns (Arabic/English headers, merged cells, date formats) into a fixed JSON schema; propose cleaning steps; write and run pandas code in the sandbox; turn the computed tables into plain-language explanations; draft recommendations that cite the specific computed numbers.
  - **Code does:** every number (aggregation, forecasting, backtest metrics, intervals, Monte Carlo scenarios).
  - **Verifier does:** after the LLM writes a narrative, a deterministic check confirms that every numeral in the text appears in, or is derivable from, the computed outputs, and blocks or flags anything else. This is a known best-practice pattern; I found no single authoritative source that names it.
- Hallucinated market facts are a separate risk. The LLM must never be the source of a macro number (inflation, FX). Those should come from an API pull (FRED, World Bank, IMF, CBE) with a stored provenance and date. I found no study that quantifies LLM hallucination of economic statistics (see Gaps).
- Cost: Claude Haiku 5.5 is cheap enough for column mapping on every upload. Claude Sonnet 5.5 is a reasonable default for analysis narratives. Per-report LLM cost should be cents (see section 7).

### Gaps
- No study was found that directly compares LLM spreadsheet analysis with and without code execution on final-answer error rates.
- No study was found that quantifies how often LLMs hallucinate economic or market statistics (for example Egyptian inflation figures).
- I did not verify whether the code-execution sandbox can pip-install statsforecast or Chronos (the "no internet" and "pip install" statements conflict).

## 4. External / market context data: what is available free or cheap, and does it help SMB forecasts?

### Takeaway
Free, programmatic macro data is plentiful: FRED (free key), the World Bank Indicators API (no key), IMF SDMX APIs, Open-Meteo weather (free for non-commercial use) and the Python `holidays` package. Google Trends has no generally available official API, only an application-based alpha, and the popular unofficial library pytrends was archived in 2025. Evidence that exogenous variables improve accuracy is positive but modest and mixed. Gains are clearest for known-in-advance drivers (holidays, promotions, weather, prices). Slow, lagged macro series help less at short horizons, except in high-inflation economies like Egypt, where deflating or FX-adjusting the target can matter more than any model choice.

### Cited Findings
- FRED: free API key issued immediately after a short form. Documented rate limit about 120 requests per 60 seconds; add `file_type=json` (XML is the default). — [freeapihub FRED tutorial](https://freeapihub.com/blog/fred-api-tutorial-python-economic-data); [qveris FRED guide](https://qveris.ai/guides/free-fred-data-api/)
- World Bank Indicators API v2 is free with no authentication. v1 is retired, and JSON responses are a two-element list (metadata, rows). Egypt's country code is EG. — [World Bank: About the Indicators API](https://datahelpdesk.worldbank.org/knowledgebase/articles/889392-about-the-indicators-api); [Apify World Bank actor docs](https://apify.com/synthetic.ia/worldbank-data)
- IMF data is available through SDMX 2.1 and SDMX 3.0 APIs on the new data.imf.org portal, with guidance for Python/R/Stata/MATLAB. Access needs a portal account. The legacy JSON service is being phased out and IFS retired. — [IMF API page](https://data.imf.org/en/Resource-Pages/IMF-API); [readimf CRAN README](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/readimf/readme/README.html)
- Open-Meteo: open-source weather API, free with no key for non-commercial use, commercial use needs a paid plan. It claims 80+ years of hourly history at about 10 km resolution (third parties say from 1940). — [open-meteo.com](https://open-meteo.com/); [Open-Meteo substack](https://openmeteo.substack.com/i/64601201/how-data-are-stored-and-made-available-through-api); [dev.to](https://dev.to/rameshchauhan/where-can-you-find-free-historical-weather-data-for-your-app-3ic3)
- Python `holidays` package: country-specific holiday sets covering about 250 country codes, subdivisions and financial markets. — [holidays on PyPI mirror](https://simple-repository.app.cern.ch/project/holidays)
- Google Trends API: official alpha announced 24 July 2025, limited and application-based, rolling 5-year window, daily/weekly/monthly/yearly aggregation, regional data, values not scaled 0–100. No published price or quota, and no GA reported as of Oct 2026. pytrends was archived on 17 April 2025. Commercial alternatives include SerpApi, DataForSEO and Bright Data. — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/google-opens-alpha-testing-for-new-trends-api-targeting-developers-and-journalists/); [konabayev 2026 landscape](https://konabayev.com/blog/google-trends-api-landscape-2026/); [scrapebadger](https://scrapebadger.com/blog/does-google-trends-have-an-api-what-to-use-in-2026)
- Egypt context: the CBE floated the pound on 6 March 2024 and raised the deposit rate to a record 27.25%. The pound went from about 31 to about 50.5 per USD. Annual inflation hit a record 38% in September 2023. CAPMAS urban inflation was 35.7% in February 2024 and 33.3% in March 2024, with monthly inflation of 11.4% in February and 1% in March. — [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/b20admd-central-bank-egypt-floats-egp-raises-interest); [Malay Mail/Reuters](https://malaymail.com/news/money/2024/03/06/egypt-pound-loses-over-third-of-value-as-bank-signals-float/121828); [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/article/egypts-inflation-eases-to-33-3-in-march/); [Enterprise](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2024/04/15/inflation-eases-in-march-following-egp-float/)
- Exogenous variables, evidence:
  - Automotive SMEs (2025): multivariate models using 30 economic, demographic and industry indicators significantly outperformed univariate models, and AutoGluon helped less technical firms. — [Springer, J. Marketing Analytics 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41270-025-00436-0)
  - Top M5 methods all used external variables (as summarised in the same study). — [Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41270-025-00436-0)
  - DeepAR with all covariates improved RMSSE by 1.8% and MASE by 6.5% over no features, and the model family mattered more than the covariates. — [ResearchGate: Robust sales forecasting with covariates](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374286376_Robust_Sales_forecasting_Using_Deep_Learning_with_Static_and_Dynamic_Covariates)
  - Market-indicator study: adding an exogenous variable helped some series (out-of-sample error 6.35 → 5.64), and LASSO selection gave only minor gains on others. — [arXiv 2406.07564](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07564v1)
  - fev-bench author: up to about 10 skill-score points of gain when top models see covariates. — [Ansari, LinkedIn](https://de.linkedin.com/in/abdulfatir)
- Chronos-2 natively accepts past-only and known-future covariates. TimesFM-3 does so natively, and TimesFM-2.5 via XReg. — [Toolify](https://www.toolify.ai/ai-model/amazon-chronos-2); [TSFM.ai](https://tsfm.ai/blog/timesfm-3-multivariate-zero-shot-forecasting)

### Inferences
- How forecasters actually use exogenous data:
  - known-future regressors (holiday and Ramadan dummies, planned promotions, planned price changes, weather forecasts), fed as covariates;
  - deflating or FX-converting the target (forecast real or USD-equivalent sales, then re-inflate under scenario inflation paths);
  - scenario inputs (a user picks an inflation or FX path for the what-if layer).
  
  Monthly or quarterly macro series as raw predictors rarely help short horizons with 24–36 points, because there are too few observations to estimate their effect.
- For Egypt and MENA, Ramadan and Eid are movable feasts that shift about 11 days a year, so a fixed-month seasonal model misattributes them. Islamic-holiday dummies are probably the single most valuable exogenous feature for retail and F&B there. That Egypt's `holidays` calendar covers Ramadan was not verified (see Gaps).
- The reliable "external context" to show users is sourced data with timestamps (FX, CPI, policy rate). Industry reports and news should be summarised by the LLM with links, never converted into numeric model inputs without human confirmation.

### Gaps
- Central Bank of Egypt and CAPMAS: I found no confirmed machine-readable API. Downloadable CBE exchange-rate and CPI series likely exist on their sites but were not verified.
- GDELT and other news APIs, commodity price APIs, and the legality of competitor-price scraping were not covered by results.
- Whether the `holidays` package's Egypt calendar includes Ramadan and Eid (Islamic-calendar estimates) was not confirmed.
- No SMB-specific study (24–36 monthly points) measured the gain from exogenous variables.

## 6. Accuracy limits and failure modes, and how to communicate uncertainty

### Takeaway
The hard limits are structural breaks (Egypt's 2023–2024 inflation and 2024 float), new products with no history, promotions without records, and tiny samples. None of these are fixed by a better model. Financial and market outcomes are close to unpredictable: in the M6 competition only about a quarter of teams beat a naive benchmark on forecasting and about 60% lost money. The product should say so plainly, show calibrated prediction intervals and backtest error, and use scenarios rather than point predictions for anything macro-driven.

### Cited Findings
- M6 (financial forecasting and investment, 163 teams): 23.3% beat the benchmark's forecast accuracy, 28.8% built better portfolios and 6.7% did both. About 60% realised negative returns, usually worse than −7% and up to −46%. Only a very small group consistently beat the market (3–4 teams depending on the version). Top forecasters built relatively inefficient portfolios, and vice versa. — [M6 paper, arXiv 2310.13357](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.13357v1); [ResearchGate version](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/386256402_The_M6_forecasting_competition_Bridging_the_gap_between_forecasting_and_investment_decisions)
- Egypt break magnitude: the pound fell from about 31 to about 50.5 per USD overnight on 6 March 2024. Monthly CPI was 11.4% in February 2024 and annual inflation peaked at 38% in September 2023. — [Malay Mail/Reuters](https://malaymail.com/news/money/2024/03/06/egypt-pound-loses-over-third-of-value-as-bank-signals-float/121828); [Enterprise](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2024/04/15/inflation-eases-in-march-following-egp-float/); [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/?p=225972)
- Capital Economics expected the currency fall to keep feeding through to prices for months after the float. — [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/article/egypts-inflation-eases-to-33-3-in-march/)
- Hyndman: minimum-sample rules are unreliable, and the data needed depends on noise and the number of parameters. — [FPP excerpt via KNIME forum](https://forum.knime.com/t/knime-decompose-signal/36511)
- StatsForecast supports conformal prediction intervals. Cross-validation windows produce out-of-sample residuals, which give distribution-free intervals for any model, including point-only ones. A calibrated 95% interval should contain the actual about 95% of the time, which can be checked on backtest output. — [Nixtla conformal prediction tutorial](https://nixtlaverse.nixtla.io/statsforecast/docs/tutorials/conformalprediction.html)
- Foundation models can fail to deliver zero-shot gains when frequencies have little training data (MOMENT authors on daily, hourly and weekly). — [MOMENT, arXiv 2402.03885](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03885)
- Statistical ensembles often beat foundation models on independent evaluation (>8.2% sCRPS, >20% MASE). Leaderboard wins do not carry over automatically. — [NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/130449)
- Disruption-era forecasting: mixed-effects models with weather and site effects handled limited, noisy foodservice data during geopolitical and economic disruption. — [PMC12872089](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12872089/)

### Inferences
- Failure modes the product must detect and disclose:
  1. Structural breaks. Flag a level or volatility shift (for example a changepoint test, or backtest error jumping after March 2024) and either model the post-break period only, or forecast in real or USD terms and re-inflate under scenarios.
  2. New products. With no history, fall back to analogue products, category share or user-entered launch curves, clearly labelled as assumptions.
  3. Promotions and stockouts. Without promo or stock flags, the model learns them as noise or seasonality. Ask the user to mark them.
  4. Small samples. With fewer than about 2 seasonal cycles, do not fit seasonal ARIMA; prefer ETS/Theta/SeasonalNaive and widen intervals.
  5. Macro and market outcomes (FX rate, competitor moves, demand shocks). Do not predict these; present them as user-chosen scenarios.
- Communication: show (a) the backtest error of the chosen model against SeasonalNaive, so users see the gain over "same as last year", (b) 80% and 95% intervals with empirical coverage from the backtest, (c) a fan chart plus 3 named scenarios (base, downside, upside) from the driver model. Avoid single-number "AI prediction" claims.
- Realistic accuracy expectations for monthly SMB revenue: no sourced SMB-specific figure was found. Typical MAPE varies widely by business type and volatility, so the product should measure it per customer, not promise it.

### Gaps
- No published accuracy figures (MAPE or MASE) for SMB monthly revenue forecasting in emerging or high-inflation markets were found.
- No empirical evaluation of how foundation models behave across the Egypt 2024 break was found.

## 7. Minimal MVP architecture and stack, estimated build time and monthly running cost

### Takeaway
A solo beginner with Claude can realistically build an MVP in about 4–8 weeks (inference) using Python (pandas, statsforecast, optional Chronos-Bolt or Chronos-2 on CPU), a Claude API layer for column mapping and narratives with numeric verification, free macro APIs, and a simple Streamlit-style web UI or HTML/PDF report. Running cost at pilot scale is dominated by a small CPU server plus a few dollars of LLM calls. No GPU and no paid forecasting API is needed.

### Cited Findings
- statsforecast provides AutoARIMA/AutoETS/AutoCES/AutoTheta with per-series cross-validated selection. — [Nixtla blog](https://www.nixtla.io/blog/statsforecast-automatic-model-selection)
- Conformal intervals and cross-validation backtesting are built into statsforecast. — [Nixtla conformal tutorial](https://nixtlaverse.nixtla.io/statsforecast/docs/tutorials/conformalprediction.html)
- Chronos models are Apache-2.0. Chronos-Bolt Tiny takes about 0.06 s per window on a desktop CPU, and Chronos-2 about 1.79 s with a long (1,032-step) history. — [chronos-forecasting repo](https://github.com/amazon-science/chronos-forecasting); [CITRAS-FM, arXiv 2606.10798](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.10798)
- Darts wraps Chronos-2, offering a single Python API for classical and foundation models. — [Darts Chronos-2 docs](https://unit8co.github.io/darts/generated_api/darts.models.forecasting.chronos2_model.html)
- An AutoGluon ensemble added value for SMEs with fewer technical resources. — [Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41270-025-00436-0)
- Claude prices: Haiku 5.5 $0.10/$0.50, Sonnet 5.5 $2/$10, Opus 5.5 $4/$20 per million tokens. Batch is 50% off, and code execution costs $0.05/hour after 1,550 free hours per month. — [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [code execution tool](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/code-execution-tool)
- TimeGPT free tier is 50 calls/month and Standard is $99/month, an optional alternative to self-hosting. — [Nixtla docs](https://docs.nixtla.io/docs/getting-started-timegpt_subscription_plans_and_pricing)
- Free data: FRED (key), World Bank (no key), IMF SDMX, Open-Meteo (free non-commercial, paid commercial). — sources in section 4.

### Inferences
- **Suggested MVP stack:**
  1. **Ingest:** XLSX/CSV upload. Claude (Haiku 5.5 or Sonnet 5.5) proposes a column mapping into a fixed schema (date, amount, product, branch, channel, cost) via structured outputs. The user confirms it in the UI, and pandas applies it deterministically.
  2. **Store:** SQLite or Postgres (for example a free or cheap managed Postgres) holding raw uploads, mapped tables, macro series with source and retrieval date, and forecast runs.
  3. **Forecast engine (Python):** statsforecast (SeasonalNaive, AutoETS, AutoTheta, AutoARIMA when n ≥ about 2 cycles), plus Chronos-Bolt-small or Chronos-2 zero-shot. A rolling-origin backtest picks or weights models per series. Conformal or native intervals, with a coverage check.
  4. **Context layer:** scheduled pulls of FX, CPI and policy rate (World Bank/IMF/FRED, CBE files where available), holiday and Ramadan dummies, and optional weather. Use these as covariates only where the backtest shows a gain, and otherwise as scenario inputs.
  5. **What-if engine:** a driver-based model in Python plus numpy Monte Carlo over the driver ranges, outputting P10/P50/P90 and three named scenarios.
  6. **LLM narrative layer:** Claude Sonnet 5.5 receives only computed JSON (forecasts, backtest metrics, scenario outputs, macro facts with sources) and writes the explanation and recommendations. A verifier checks every number against the JSON, and the LLM never sees "make up a forecast" prompts.
  7. **Front end:** Streamlit/Gradio, or FastAPI plus a simple HTML template rendered to PDF. The report shows a fan chart, a backtest table versus SeasonalNaive, assumptions and caveats.
- **Rough build time:** 4–8 weeks part-time-to-full-time for a beginner with Claude writing most code: about 1 week ingest and mapping, 1–2 weeks forecasting and backtest, 1 week what-if engine, 1 week LLM layer and verification, 1–2 weeks UI, report and testing on 3–5 real SMB datasets. Connectors (Foodics, Xero, QuickBooks) add about 1–2 weeks each because of OAuth and approvals. This is an unsourced estimate.
- **Rough monthly running cost at pilot scale (10–50 SMBs, monthly refresh), unsourced estimate:**
  - one small CPU VPS or PaaS instance, roughly low tens of USD/month (no sourced price);
  - LLM costs, per report: at about 30K input and 5K output tokens on Sonnet 5.5, ≈ $0.06 + $0.05 ≈ $0.11; column mapping on Haiku 5.5 ≈ <$0.01 per upload. Even 500 reports a month is about $55, and less with caching or batching;
  - data APIs $0;
  - optional TimeGPT $0–99.
  
  Total plausibly about $20–100/month before scale. Commercial Open-Meteo use, Xero above 5 connections and QuickBooks above Builder add cost.
- **Do not build in an MVP:** MMM, causal-ML attribution, LLM-generated forecasts, auto-scraped competitor prices, or claims of predicting FX or market moves.

### Gaps
- No sourced hosting price figures (VPS/PaaS) were gathered.
- No sourced evidence on actual build times for comparable solo-built forecasting MVPs.
- Memory and latency of Chronos-2 on small cloud CPU instances with short (≤36-point) contexts were not measured in any source found.
