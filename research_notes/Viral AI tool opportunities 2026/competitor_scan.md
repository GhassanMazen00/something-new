## 1. Old-rent law (الإيجار القديم, Law 164/2025) calculator / explainer: who already does it, and how big is the gap?

### Takeaway
Basic "how much will my rent go up" calculators already exist and are widely syndicated (the main one is Youm7's widget, republished by at least four other sites, plus Banker.news and Legalix). But from the descriptions, none of them does the whole job: zone multiplier, then the legal minimum, then 15% compounding, then the lease end date, then your rights. I also found no tool that tells a tenant **which zone their street falls in**. That information is scattered across governorate decrees, the official gazette and newspaper street lists. The gap is real but narrow, it matters mostly at certain times of year, and the law is still being challenged in the Constitutional Court.

**Rules any calculator must encode (from secondary sources; check against the law text)**
- Residential units: premium (متميزة) zones pay 20x the current legal rent, minimum 1,000 EGP. Medium (متوسطة) zones pay 10x, minimum 400 EGP. Economic (اقتصادية) zones pay 10x, minimum 250 EGP. — [Youm7 2025-08-06](https://www.youm7.com/story/2025/8/6/%D8%AE%D9%84%D8%B5%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D9%83%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%83%D9%8A%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D8%AD%D8%AA%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AC%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%AA%D8%AD%D9%82%D8%A9-%D9%88%D9%81%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%8B-%D9%84%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D9%8A%D8%AC%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%85/7080190); [Ahram Gate](https://gate.ahram.org.eg/News/5474100.aspx)
- Non-residential units leased to individuals pay 5x the current legal rent. — [ADSERO](https://adsero.me/egypt-enacts-major-reform-to-old-rental-laws/); [Amwal Al Ghad](https://amwalalghad.com/2025/08/04/%D9%83%D9%8A%D9%81-%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%8F%D8%AD%D8%AA%D8%B3%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D9%8A%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D9%8A%D8%AC%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%84/)
- Until a zone is classified, the tenant pays a flat interim rent of 250 EGP a month. — [Egypt Daily News](https://egyptdailynews.com/annual-old-rent-increase-begins-september-1/) (as summarized in search results)
- The yearly increase is 15%, compounded, and applied to the new legal rent, not the old one. The first increase took effect on 2026-09-01. — [Matn News](https://www.matnnews.com/300453); [Egypt Daily News](https://egyptdailynews.com/annual-old-rent-increase-begins-september-1/). Sources disagree on wording: ADSERO says only that "all rental values shall be increased annually by 15%". — [ADSERO](https://adsero.me/egypt-enacts-major-reform-to-old-rental-laws/)
- The transition period is 7 years for residential and 5 years for non-residential, counted from when the law took effect. HAQQ puts the end of residential contracts around August 2032. — [Dostor](https://www.dostor.org/5719117); [HAQQ blog](https://www.haqq.ai/blog/ijar-qadim-ikhla-misr)

- The simple "current rent → +15% for 7 years" calculator is already commoditized. Egypt's highest-traffic news site owns it and other sites republish it, so a copy would struggle to rank or spread.
- What is not served, based on the evidence: (a) a correct two-step calculation (zone multiplier and minimum first, then 15% compounding, interim 250 EGP, 5x for non-residential, residential vs. non-residential end dates); (b) **typing a street or district and getting its zone** across governorates, since that data sits in gazette PDFs and one-off news articles; (c) a plain-language rights explainer covering eviction cases, appeals against committee decisions, and alternative housing. Option (b) is the clearest defensible gap, but it means a lot of manual data entry from scanned decrees, which is a heavy job for a solo beginner. A sensible start would be Cairo, Giza and Alexandria.
- Demand comes in bursts. It peaked around the 2026-09-01 first increase and the 2026-10-12 alternative-housing deadline. The next natural spike is September 2027 (second 15% step), plus any Constitutional Court ruling. A tool launched in November 2026 would miss this year's peak.
- Liability and maintenance risk: a court ruling or an amendment could change the formula. The tool would need clear "unofficial" disclaimers and a visible "last updated" date.

### Gaps
- I could not open the Youm7, Banker.news or Legalix calculators, so whether they handle zones is inferred from descriptions, not tested.
- I found no traffic data for these widgets and no total count of old-rent units or households (often cited as millions, but I found no source in this scan).
- I did not find any Facebook, Telegram or WhatsApp old-rent bot. Facebook groups were not searchable from here, and there may be tenant/landlord groups with pinned calculators.
- I could not confirm that every one of the 27 governorates has published its classification, or whether any governorate site hosts a searchable map.

## 2. Arabic "is this a scam?" checker (messages, links, numbers, screenshots; InstaPay/bank/WhatsApp scams in Egypt): who already does it, and do the global tools support Arabic?

### Inferences
- What is not served: a **free, no-install, Arabic and Egyptian-dialect checker** that takes a pasted message, a screenshot (OCR) or a phone number. It would explain the verdict using a curated library of Egyptian patterns (fake InstaPay or bank staff, "your Vodafone Cash wallet is suspended", fake delivery and customs fees, job and Telegram task scams). Its most useful output would be the exact next steps: the bank hotline, CBE guidance, NTRA 155, and the Ministry of Interior cybercrime channel. Nothing above combines all of this.
- Link scanners and Truecaller already cover "is this link or number known bad". The new value is in **explaining the wording of the message**, written for non-technical relatives. That is what would get shared in family WhatsApp groups.
- The competitive risk is real but slow. Google's Arabic-capable Scam Detection is limited to Google Messages and Android, and WhatsApp's alert is still in testing. Neither reads a screenshot forwarded from another app, and neither gives Egypt-specific next steps.
- Main risks: false "safe" verdicts (the tool should never say "safe", only "warning signs found / not found"), and data-privacy expectations (the indie projects stress that processing stays in the browser).

## 3. University admission (تنسيق) predictor and Egyptian Baccalaureate (البكالوريا المصرية) track advisor: who already does it?

- A new tansik predictor would be a me-too product competing with Shabbabek's already-branded "AI assistant" and several SEO-tuned sites. Accuracy is capped by the same public data (past cutoffs) everyone uses. Verdict: well served. Don't build it.
- For the Baccalaureate, the only possibly open niche is a **"track → which colleges/majors → what I'd need" explorer** built from the ministries' published mappings. It would sit next to the official track-selection service and the Fund's aptitude test. A real admission-chance predictor is impossible until the first cohort's scores and cutoffs exist (that cohort hasn't graduated; see Gaps).
- Both tools are strongly seasonal: tansik in July–September, track choice around spring.

## 4. Consumer contract red-flag checker in Arabic (rental, employment, off-plan real estate) under Egyptian law: is there a free tool, and are general assistants the de facto answer?

### Takeaway
The market is **crowded on the B2B / lawyer side**: HAQQ, Lexa AI, XS Lawyer, Laiwyer, LegalMind Egypt, Clauze.AI, Laika. There are also several consumer-facing Arabic "AI lawyer" apps with free tiers or trials (مستشارك الذكي/jusxai, محامي سمارت, Qaanoon, Shwra, SmartLegAI, Kanoni AI), but their quality is unverified. ChatGPT (about 70–75% AI-chatbot share in Egypt) and Gemini are the de facto default for "read my contract". I found **no free, consumer-focused, Egyptian-law checklist tool specifically for rental, employment or off-plan contracts**, but it is hard to stand out from "paste it into ChatGPT", and the legal risk is higher than for the other candidates. Gap: narrow.

### Inferences
- B2B legal AI covering Egyptian law is crowded and funded, so it is not a beginner's market. Consumer Arabic "AI lawyer" apps exist in numbers, but none I found is clearly a **free, single-purpose "upload your rental, job or off-plan contract and get a red-flag list"** tool tied to Egyptian statutes (Civil Code, Labour Law 14/2025, old-rent law, real-estate developer rules).
- The honest competitor is ChatGPT. Advantages a beginner could offer: a curated Egyptian checklist per contract type ("does it name the delivery date and the penalty for late delivery?", "is the deposit refundable?", "are there probation-period limits?"), legal references in each answer, and no sign-up. Unless the checklist is carefully researched, it adds little over a general assistant.
- It is the riskiest of the four (people may make binding decisions on the output) and the least viral. People rarely share their contracts, though they might share "10 red flags in off-plan contracts".

### Inferences
- Overlap by candidate:
  - **#1 old-rent**: Karnak Chat or the future legal assistant could answer generic questions about the law. Neither has the street-to-zone data or a deterministic calculator. The Digital Egypt chatbot only covers the alternative-housing application. Low overlap.
  - **#2 scam checker**: شات EGYPT checks rumors against official statements, not scams. Karnak Chat could judge a pasted message like any LLM. Low to medium overlap.
  - **#3 tansik/Baccalaureate**: SIA is a subject tutor, not an advisor. No overlap.
  - **#4 contract checker**: Karnak Chat (free and Egyptian-dialect) plus the announced legal and regulatory assistant are the closest government overlap, so medium risk once the legal assistant launches.
- The government AI apps are pilots with no published usage figures and no independent evaluation, so they don't currently set a high bar. They also signal that the state intends to occupy "legal and government guidance".
- Karnak-40B (open weights) and BelMasry (free STT/TTS) are potential building blocks, but a beginner would more likely use a hosted commercial LLM API.

### Gaps

- I could not find the name, URL or launch status of the MCIT "legal and regulatory guidance" or "smart judicial" assistant.
- "Sia" as a Digital Egypt services assistant (as phrased in the brief) could not be confirmed. All sources found describe SIA as an education tutor.

## 6. Bottom line: how big is the gap for each candidate?

### Takeaway
Ranked by how unserved each is: **(1) Arabic/Egyptian scam-message checker: largest real gap**, evergreen need, but Google and WhatsApp are advancing. **(2) Old-rent calculator: basic version already taken by Youm7. A gap remains for a correct zone-aware calculator plus street-to-zone lookup and rights explainer**, but demand is seasonal and the data entry is heavy. **(3) Contract red-flag checker: narrow gap**, crowded field and ChatGPT is the default. **(4) Tansik predictor: no meaningful gap**. Baccalaureate advice is partly served officially and there is no cutoff data yet.

### Inferences
- For a solo beginner who wants a free, viral, genuinely needed tool, the scam checker has the best mix: evergreen demand, an obvious sharing loop in family WhatsApp groups, and no direct free Egyptian competitor. The old-rent tool is a good **second** project, or a scoped-down first one (e.g., a Cairo+Giza street lookup plus a correct calculator) timed for the September 2027 increase or any Constitutional Court ruling.
- I'd advise against a tansik predictor (saturated) and against a contract checker as a first project (crowded, high liability, low virality).

### Gaps
- Without traffic data for competitors (Youm7 widget, Shabbabek, Tansiky, Tabayyun), the size of each audience can't be measured. These rankings are judgments based on what tools exist, not on measured demand.
