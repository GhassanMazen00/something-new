# Global consumer needs that AI + public APIs make newly solvable (2025-2026), and how solo-built web tools went viral

Research date: 2026-10-26. Method note: WebFetch failed with DNS errors on several domains (wikipedia.org, dev.to, popsci.com, levels.io), so most findings below come from search-result summaries of the cited pages rather than full-page reads. Figures marked "self-reported" or "secondary" should be re-checked against the primary source before being quoted in a final report.

## Q1. Which consumer problems are growing fast in 2025-2026 and are badly served? (scale, news, existing tools, gaps)

### Takeaway
Fraud is the largest, fastest-growing, best-documented problem: US-reported fraud losses hit records in 2025 (FTC about $15.9B; FBI IC3 about $20.9B), older adults' losses rose about 59%, and AI-enabled scams (voice clones, deepfakes) are now a measurable category. The free consumer tools that exist are mostly English-only or US-only (Norton Genie, Bitdefender Scamio, SynthID Detector, ChatGPT Health, California DROP, Fight Health Insurance). That leaves a clear opening for a multilingual, Arabic-first "check this message / protect my family" web tool. Health-insurance appeals, US data-broker removal and leaked-password checks are already served by free or cheap tools in the US, so they are weaker opportunities unless localized.

### Cited Findings

#### Problem 1: Scam / phishing messages (SMS, WhatsApp, email, fake sites). Largest scale.
- FTC: consumers reported about $15.9B in fraud losses in 2025, a record, from about 3 million reports (2.6M reports and more than $12B in 2024). About half of 2025 losses came from investment scams — [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/?p=269398); [AOL/Yahoo](https://www.aol.com/articles/americans-lost-record-15-9-135921819.html); [MoneyTalksNews](https://www.moneytalksnews.com/americans-lost-a-record-billion-to-scams-in-ftc-says/)
- FTC: imposter scams were the most-reported category (nearly one-third of reports), with more than $3.5B lost in 2025 — [FTC press release, June 2026](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/06/ftc-data-show-people-reported-losing-3-point-5-billion-imposter-scams-2025); [Digital Watch](https://dig.watch/updates/ftc-imposter-scams-2025-losses)
- FTC: losses reported by adults 60+ went from about $600M in 2020 to about $3B in 2025, roughly 5x — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/fraud-prevention/2026/consumer-fraud-losses-quintupled-since-2020-ftc-says/)
- FBI IC3 2025: 1,008,597 complaints (the first year over 1 million) and about $20.9B in losses, up 26% on 2024 — [Hoodline](https://hoodline.com/2026/04/cyber-crooks-bleed-americans-for-record-20-billion-fbi-says/); [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/fbi-releases-its-2025-internet-crime-7569349/)
- FBI IC3 2025, adults 60+: 201,266 complaints and about $7.7B lost (+59% YoY), averaging $38,500 per victim. More than 12,400 lost over $100k each. Tech/customer-support scams cost seniors $1.04B. Seniors filed more than 3,100 complaints referencing AI, with losses above $352M — [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/fbi-ftc-report-2025-losses.html); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/fbi-seniors-cybercrime-2025/)
- FBI: crypto-related fraud losses hit a record $11.4B in 2025, and seniors accounted for $4.4B of that — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/397134/fbi-says-crypto-related-fraud-losses-hit-record-11-4-billion-in-2025-with-seniors-bearing-the-brunt)
- Arabic-speaking market signal (UAE, GASA/BioCatch 2024, about 2,000 respondents): instant messaging is the main scam channel and WhatsApp the most misused platform. 56% get a scam attempt at least monthly, about 27% lost money (average $2,194), only 9% fully recovered it, and only 30% reported to police. Half believed they had met an AI-based scam in the past year — [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/b6f93te-2024-state-scams-uae-survey-reveals-65-scam); [BioCatch press release](https://www.biocatch.com/press-release/uae-residents-lose-millions-to-scams-2024); [Nukta](https://nukta.com/56-of-uae-residents-face-monthly-scam-attempts-survey-finds)
- Competitor: Norton Genie (free AI scam detector app). Its store listing says it is "currently available only in English and does not evaluate for scams in other languages" but can still analyze URLs. The listing seen dates from 2024, so it may be outdated — [Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6448706515); [Norton product page](https://us.norton.com/products/genie-scam-detector)
- Competitor: Bitdefender Scamio, a free chatbot on web, WhatsApp, Messenger and Discord. AlternativeTo lists English as its only language (third-party listing, not confirmed by Bitdefender) — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/bitdefender-scamio/about/)
- Underreporting: the FTC says only about 4.8% of scam victims report — [Bitdefender blog citing FTC](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/blog/hotforsecurity/ftc-americans-lost-12-5-billion-to-scams-last-year)

#### Problem 2: AI voice-clone and deepfake "family emergency" scams
- FBI PSA I-120324-PSA (Dec 3, 2024) tells families to agree a secret word or phrase to verify callers, because criminals use AI audio of relatives asking for urgent money or ransom. It also advises limiting public voice samples — [Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/blog/hotforsecurity/fbi-alarm-ai-scams); [CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/news/how-to-protect-your-family-from-ai-voice-clones-claiming-to-be-you)
- January 2026 local-TV syndication (Gray TV stations) reported AI voice-cloning "fake kidnapping" calls targeting families — [KTIV](https://www.ktiv.com/2026/01/23/ai-voice-cloning-scams-target-families-with-fake-kidnapping-calls)
- Survey benchmarks, all via aggregators (primaries not retrieved): McAfee 2023 found about 10% had received an AI voice-clone message and 77% of those lost money. Starling Bank 2024 (UK) found 28% targeted in the past year and 46% had never heard of the scam. Hiya Q4 2024 found 31% of US consumers had met a deepfake voice call — [SQ Magazine (aggregator)](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/ai-voice-cloning-fraud-statistics/); [Voxbooster (aggregator)](https://voxbooster.com/blog/voice-cloning-family-fraud-statistics-2026)
- Gallup / Stop Scams Alliance (5,173 US adults): 12% of successful scams in 2025 involved AI or deepfakes (secondary report) — [GadgetReview](https://www.gadgetreview.com/?p=390082)
- Competitor: Hiya Deepfake Voice Detector, a free Chrome extension (Oct 2024) that scores a voice sample of about 1 second. It is audio-only, reportedly needs a verified email, and the vendor claims up to 99% accuracy, but PCMag testing found mixed results — [Hiya press release](https://www.hiya.com/press-releases/hiya-launches-free-web-tool-to-help-people-identify-deepfakes-in-run-up-to-us-election-and-beyond); [CO/AI summary of PCMag](https://getcoai.com/news/this-chrome-extension-detects-ai-deepfakes-in-seconds)
- Conflicting or weak stat: a blog attributes "22,364 AI-related complaints / $893M" to FBI 2025 data. Not verified against IC3 — [Voxbooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/voice-cloning-family-fraud-statistics-2026)

#### Problem 3: Job hunting in an AI-flooded market (job scams, ghost jobs, ATS noise)
- FTC: job scams were the third-most-reported fraud type in 2024, with about $750M lost (about $2,250 per victim). Another FTC-based summary gives $501M for 2024 (up from $90M in 2020). The two figures are unreconciled. In H1 2025, reports of online job scams rose 19% YoY with almost $300M lost. Gamified "task scams" were about 40% of 2024 job-scam reports — [WKNO/FTC](https://www.wknofm.org/show/protecting-your-money/2026-07-14/ftc-warns-job-scams-are-on-the-rise); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/11/17/ftc-data-confirms-job-offer-text-scams-more-common-now); [Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/blog/hotforsecurity/ftc-americans-lost-12-5-billion-to-scams-last-year); [HotHardware](https://hothardware.com/news/ftc-massive-spike-online-game-like-job-scams)
- Ghost jobs: a 2025 Greenhouse study found at least 1 in 5 US job postings is fake or never filled. iHire's 2025 report found 39.3% of job seekers named "fake, fraudulent, or 'ghost' jobs" their biggest job-board challenge. Ontario passed a law against the practice, effective January 2026 — [Wikipedia: Ghost job](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_job); [iHire](https://www.ihireaccounting.com/resourcecenter/jobseeker/pages/ask-a-career-advisor-what-is-a-ghost-job)
- Volume: LinkedIn applications rose 45% to about 11,000 per minute (NYT, June 2025), partly driven by AI tools that keyword-stuff résumés. 73% of HR professionals say fewer than half of applicants meet basic requirements — [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/06/24/2025/recruiters-swamped-with-ai-generated-job-applications); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/ai-job-applications-linkedin/)

#### Problem 4: Health-insurance denials and medical bills (US). Large pain, but free tools exist.
- KFF: HealthCare.gov insurers denied about 19% of in-network claims in 2024 (about 85M claims), and 37% of out-of-network claims. Insurer rates ranged from 3% to 36% (report published Mar 24, 2026). For 2023, fewer than 1% of denied claims were appealed and insurers upheld 56% of appeals — [KFF 2024](https://www.kff.org/private-insurance/claims-denials-and-appeals-in-aca-marketplace-plans-in-2024/); [KFF 2023](https://www.kff.org/private-insurance/claims-denials-and-appeals-in-aca-marketplace-plans-in-2023/)
- Competitor (solo-built, free, open source): Fight Health Insurance by Holden Karau. You upload a denial letter, it drafts several appeal letters, and it removes names and addresses. It is free apart from an optional $5 fax service, and US-only — [Business Insider NL](https://businessinsider.nl/shes-worked-at-nearly-every-faang-company-in-her-free-time-she-built-a-free-tool-to-appeal-health-insurance-denials/); [BGR](https://bgr.com/tech/this-free-site-uses-ai-to-help-you-fight-health-insurance-claim-denials/); [Quartz](https://qz.com/fight-health-insurance-denials-appeals-ai-1851733712)
- Competitor: Counterforce Health is free for patients and clinics, writes an appeal letter in about 2 minutes, and has a voice agent ("Maxwell") that calls insurers. It is grant-funded ($280k) and claims about 20,000 people helped (self-reported) — [Axios Raleigh](https://www.axios.com/local/raleigh/2025/08/20/using-ai-to-fight-back-against-insurance-denials-counteforce); [WECT](https://www.wect.com/2025/07/25/ai-tool-that-helps-patients-battle-insurance-claim-denials-got-its-start-wilmingon)

#### Problem 5: Understanding medical / lab results
- KFF (Mar 2026 poll, n=1,343): 32% of US adults used AI chatbots for health information in the past year, on par with social media. 65% cite wanting quick advice as a major reason, and about 1 in 5 cite having no provider or no appointment available — [KFF news release](https://www.kff.org/health-information-trust/poll-1-in-3-adults-are-turning-to-ai-chatbots-for-health-information-equaling-the-share-who-use-social-media-for-health/); [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ai/32-of-adults-turn-to-ai-for-health-advice-kff/)
- KFF 2024: 56% of AI users were not confident AI health information is accurate, so there is a trust gap — [KFF 2024 poll](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/kff-health-misinformation-tracking-poll-artificial-intelligence-and-health-information/)
- Competitor: "Health in ChatGPT" rolled out nationally around July 23, 2026. It connects Apple Health and medical records and compares lab results over time, and is available on Free, Go, Plus and Pro. It is US adults only and "not available in other countries yet". These are secondary sources, and the launch date conflicts with an earlier waitlist phase — [Tech-Now guide](https://tech-now.io/en/blogs/health-in-chatgpt/); [FoneArena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/488171/openai-health-chatgpt-features.html/amp); [OpenAI waitlist page](https://openai.com/blog/introducing-chatgpt-health)

#### Problem 6: Understanding legal / financial documents (leases, contracts, terms)
- Regulatory caution: the FTC finalized an order (Feb 11, 2025) requiring DoNotPay to pay $193,000 and stop calling itself "the world's first robot lawyer". It had not tested its AI against human lawyers — [Slashdot/FTC](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/25/02/11/1932223/); [MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2296777)

#### Problem 7: Privacy (data brokers) and account security
- California DROP, the free one-request deletion across 580+ registered brokers, launched Jan 1, 2026. It had 242k sign-ups in 8 weeks and more than 300k by June 2026. Brokers began processing on Aug 1, 2026. It is California-only, and critics note there is no monitoring of re-listing — [CalPrivacy](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/06/privacy-momentum-builds-300000-californians-sign-up-for-drop-as-registered-data-brokers-hit-a-record-high/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/californias-drop-platform-hits-242-000-sign-ups-in-its-first-8-weeks/); [DeleteMe blog (vendor)](https://joindeleteme.com/blog/californias-drop-is-live-how-to-use-it/)
- Paid incumbents: DeleteMe $129/yr, Incogni $95.88/yr (Standard), Optery $39 to $249/yr with a free tier. Consumer Reports' 2024 test ranked Optery Ultimate top at 68% of profiles removed in 4 months — [Security.org](https://www.security.org/data-removal/deleteme/); [Tech Insider comparison](https://tech-insider.org/deleteme-vs-incogni-vs-optery-2026/)
- Leaked passwords are already well served for free by Have I Been Pwned (see Q2) — [Cloudflare case study](https://www.cloudflare.com/case-studies/troy-hunt)

#### Problem 8: Subscription traps

Other state and federal auto-renewal laws still apply — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/07/ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule-vacated-ahead-of-planned-july-14-effective-date); [Kirkland](https://www.kirkland.com/publications/kirkland-alert/2025/07/eighth-circuit-blocks-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule)

C+R Research (2022, widely recirculated): people guessed $86/mo against an actual $219/mo, 42% forgot they were still paying, and 86% use autopay — [Cord Cutters News citing CNET](https://cordcuttersnews.com/americans-spend-over-200-a-year-on-unused-subscriptions-here-is-how-to-easily-stop-them/); [NBC Washington](https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/business/money-report/consumers-spend-an-average-133-more-each-month-on-subscriptions-than-they-realize-study-shows/3067565/)

### Inferences
- **Ranked shortlist for a free AI web tool**, judged on scale, growth, how weak competitors are and how buildable it is for a beginner:
  1. **Multilingual, Arabic-first "Is this a scam?" checker.** You paste text or upload a screenshot of an SMS, WhatsApp message or email, plus an optional URL. You get a plain-language verdict, the red flags explained, and what to do next. This has the biggest scale, the fastest growth, and incumbents that are English-only (Norton Genie, Scamio per listings). WhatsApp is the main channel in the Gulf. An LLM with vision plus URL-reputation APIs makes it buildable.
  2. **Family AI-voice-clone defense kit.** A guided safe-word setup, a "practice call" drill or quiz, and a one-page explainer for grandparents in Arabic and English. Detection tools are unreliable (Hiya's mixed PCMag results), so the FBI's own recommendation, a safe word, is the realistic product. It has a built-in invite loop (see Q3).
  3. **Job-posting scam and ghost-job checker.** Paste a job ad or recruiter message and get a risk score with reasons. FTC job-scam reports are rising and 1 in 5 postings may be ghost jobs. It overlaps with #1, so it could be a mode of the same tool.
  4. **"Is this image/claim real?" helper for forwarded content.** Combines SynthID (if an API is available, unverified), reverse-image search and LLM claim analysis. SynthID is English-only and watermark-only, which leaves room for an Arabic explainer layer. The risk is overclaiming accuracy (GPTZero-style false positives).
  5. **Lab-result explainer** for non-US or Arabic users. Demand is strong (32% of US adults already use AI for health), and ChatGPT Health is US-only. Liability and privacy risk is high, and the output is not shareable.
  6. **Document explainer** (lease, contract, bill, insurance policy) with "questions to ask" output. Demand is plausible, but the DoNotPay order shows the legal-claims risk. Position it as an explainer, not a lawyer.
  7. **Subscription-waste finder** (upload a bank statement CSV or PDF, get recurring charges and cancellation links). The pain is quantified ($205/yr dormant), but uploading financial data raises trust problems, and good funded US apps likely exist (not verified).
  8. **Insurance-denial appeal letters.** Weak opportunity in the US because two free tools exist (Fight Health Insurance, Counterforce). It is a possible template for non-US markets only if appeal processes exist there.
  9. **Data-broker removal.** Weak for a beginner: it is US-centric, California now has free DROP, and paid incumbents do the hard manual work.
- The common gap across almost every free incumbent is **language and geography** (English-only or US-only), not missing technology. An Arabic + English tool with dialect-aware prompts is a real differentiator, especially for scam messages, which are culture-specific (fake bank, delivery and government-fine SMS).
- Accuracy is the main product risk across fraud, health, legal and AI detection. The safest framing is "explain red flags, recommend verification steps" rather than "certify safe".

### Gaps
- No primary-source FBI IC3 PDF or FTC Consumer Sentinel data book was read (WebFetch DNS failures). Figures are from press and secondary summaries. The FTC June 2026 press-release figure is the most primary item.
- No 2025-2026 scam data was found for Saudi Arabia or other Arab markets (Egypt was excluded by scope). The UAE data is from 2024.
- Norton Genie and Scamio language support is from store and third-party listings (possibly outdated). Their current Arabic support was not verified on the vendors' own sites.
- Not researched in depth because of the tool-call budget: accessibility (e.g., Be My Eyes), government forms and immigration paperwork, language-barrier tools, lease or contract tool landscape and scale data, Rocket Money and other subscription-tool pricing, Jobscan or other ATS-checker pricing, Pew Research data.
- Whether SynthID Detector has a public API for third-party tools is unknown.
- No reliable evidence was found on how much medical-bill errors cost consumers (commonly cited "80% of bills have errors" claims were not verified and are excluded).

## Q2. Case studies (2020-2026) of solo or tiny-team web tools that went viral: mechanics, traffic, costs, aftermath

### Takeaway
Viral solo tools share a recognizable recipe: one input, one striking output, and a share mechanic built in. The input is often an OAuth login or a pasted text or username, there is no signup wall, and the output is spoiler-free or identity-expressing (emoji grid, receipt, poster, roast). The launch rides an existing conversation: a viral tweet, a news event, a season like Spotify Wrapped, or a fresh fear like ChatGPT cheating. Costs were handled with static or no-backend designs, edge caching, sponsored hosting, free-tier APIs, ads or an optional paid add-on. Many crashed or ran out of credits at peak, which is the norm. Several pure "fun" categories (roast tools, chat-wrapped apps) are now saturated and rarely break out.

### Cited Findings

#### Wordle (Josh Wardle, solo, launched Oct 2021). The canonical share mechanic.
- 90 players on Nov 1, 2021, then more than 300,000 just over two months later — [TheyGotAcquired](https://theygotacquired.com/?p=1008); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2022/01/31/1077089945/nyt-wordle)
- Wardle saw New Zealand players hand-typing colored-square emoji results and automated it into a copy-to-clipboard grid. The grid shows how you did without spoiling the word, and the grids "flooded Twitter and Facebook" — [Clemson Social Media Listening Center](https://blogs.clemson.edu/smlc/the-new-york-times-buys-wordle/); [Westminster World](https://westminsterworld.shorthandstories.com/what-is-wordle-and-why-is-it-popular-all-of-a-sudden/index.html)
- One puzzle a day creates a shared ritual and scarcity. The NYT bought it on Jan 31, 2022 for "low seven figures" — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2022/01/31/1077089945/nyt-wordle); [Edinburgh News](https://www.edinburghnews.scotsman.com/news/world/wordle-snapped-up-by-the-new-york-times-in-low-seven-figure-deal-3550054)

#### Receiptify (Michelle Liu, student, solo, Sept 2020)
- You log in with Spotify, pick a time range, and get your top tracks as a shopping receipt (song length as the "price"). The idea was borrowed from the @albumreceipts Instagram account — [Bandwagon](https://bandwagon.asia/articles/receiptify-turns-your-top-tracks-on-spotify-into-shopping-receipts-michelle-liu); [Yorkshire Post](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/read-this/heres-how-people-are-making-receipts-of-their-top-spotify-tracks-2981151)
- The launch tweet got about 36k likes and 31k retweets (another outlet says more than 25k RTs in a day). Usage claims conflict: "a million users in the first 24 hours" (Tartan interview) against "used more than one million times in its first few months" (CMU Studio). She upgraded to paid Heroku to handle traffic — [CMU STUDIO](https://studioforcreativeinquiry.org/project/receiptify); [Tartan interview PDF](https://studioforcreativeinquiry.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Interview_with_Michelle_Liu_The_Tartan.pdf)

#### Instafest (Anshay Saboo, USC student, solo, Nov 26, 2022)
- Spotify login generates a fictional 3-day festival poster of your top artists, plus a "Basic Score" rating how niche your taste is. It launched days before Spotify Wrapped. About a week in, Saboo said Google Analytics showed more than 16M users, with more than 5M posters generated (TechCrunch via ABC10). He had hoped for 1M users and spread it via friends and his own posts — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/28/instafest-app-lets-you-create-your-own-festival-lineup-from-spotify); [ABC10](https://www.abc10.com/article/entertainment/instafest-create-festival-lineup-from-your-top-artists-app-generates-posters/521-fea3d823-c191-40e7-a274-2432d7716542); [USC Today](https://today.usc.edu/instafest-usc-student/); [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/sites/instafest)

#### GPTZero (Edward Tian, Princeton student, solo, Jan 2, 2023). Timeliness and fear.
- Built over winter break to detect ChatGPT-written essays, a month after ChatGPT's launch, at the peak of fear about cheating. 2,000+ beta signups in hours and 30,000+ users in the first week. Traffic crashed Streamlit, its free host, and Streamlit then gave it more resources. NPR coverage amplified it — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2023/01/09/1147549845/gptzero-ai-chatgpt-edward-tian-plagiarism); [Wikipedia: GPTZero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPTZero); [Daily Beast](https://www.thedailybeast.com/princeton-student-edward-tian-built-gptzero-to-detect-ai-written-essays)

#### Human or Not? (AI21 Labs, small company team, May-June 2023). A gamified Turing test.
- Two-minute chat, then guess "human or bot?". More than 1.5M users in a month (paper) and later more than 2M participants and 15M conversations. Players were right 68% of the time overall and 60% against bots. It is not solo, but it shows the "test yourself" mechanic at scale — [arXiv 2305.20010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20010v1); [AI21 results (archived)](https://gwern.net/doc/www/www.ai21.com/720e6f1aad476d5f5fe5b0ee8d7dcd44c5e83901.html)

#### Infinite Craft (Neal Agarwal, solo, Jan/Feb 2024). LLM-powered browser game.
- Players combine elements into new AI-generated ones, using Llama 2 via Together AI. The launch post on X got more than 15M views, 11k reposts and 37k likes in a week. Players shared unique crafts and failed attempts, and it spread on Twitch and YouTube. Running costs were not found — [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/sites/infinite-craft); [FlowingData](https://flowingdata.com/2024/02/02/infinite-craft-with-large-language-model/); [Wikipedia: Infinite Craft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_Craft)

#### WikiTok (Isaac Gemal, solo, Feb 3-4, 2025). AI-assisted build that rode a tweet.
- The idea came from a viral tweet by Tyler Angert ("all of wikipedia on a single, scrollable page"), with James Cham suggesting infinite scroll. ### Inferences
- **Mechanics that recur across the winners:**
  1. **Zero-friction input.** Spotify OAuth (Receiptify, Instafest), no input at all (WikiTok, Wordle), or paste text (GPTZero). No account creation before value.
  2. **A visual, screenshot-ready output** sized for social sharing: a receipt, a poster, an emoji grid. The output carries the brand and URL, so every share is an ad.
  3. **A comparison or score hook** (Instafest's "Basic Score", Wordle's guess count, Human or Not's accuracy) that invites "what did you get?".
  4. **Riding an existing wave**: a viral tweet (WikiTok, Jungle), a seasonal moment (Instafest before Wrapped), a news peg or fear (GPTZero after ChatGPT, Fight Health Insurance during the Dec 2024 insurance outrage).
  5. **A single launch channel with a fitting community**: Hacker News (WikiTok), a niche subreddit (FormulaBot), the founder's X audience (fly.pieter.com), press (GPTZero, Fight Health Insurance).
  6. **Speed**: built in hours or days, launched while the wave was live.
- **Cost-handling patterns usable by a beginner:** no backend or static hosting (WikiTok, Wordle), aggressive caching (HIBP, and likely LLM-result caching in Infinite Craft, though not verified here), host sponsorship after a crash (GPTZero/Streamlit), a free core with an optional paid add-on (Fight Health Insurance's $5 fax; fly.pieter.com's F-16), ads or sponsors once there is traffic, and billing alerts (HIBP's bill shock). Paid AI APIs are the dominant variable cost (restorePhotos). Free-tier LLMs, rate limits and caching identical queries matter.
- **Aftermath patterns:** acquisition (Wordle), a startup (GPTZero; its funding was not verified here), a move to a paywall (roomGPT), staying free and open source (WikiTok, Fight Health Insurance), or fading after the spike (fly.pieter.com revenue claims vary widely). Utility tools (HIBP, Photopea) show durable traffic, while novelty tools spike and decay.
- For a beginner without an audience, the fly.pieter.com model (relying on 600k followers) is not replicable. WikiTok, GPTZero and FormulaBot (a timely idea plus HN, Reddit or press) are the realistic templates.

### Gaps
- Traffic numbers for nearly all cases are self-reported or from secondary press. No independent analytics (Similarweb etc.) were retrieved.
- Infinite Craft's running costs and caching strategy were not found in sources. The "First Discovery" sharing feature is widely known but was not verified in this research.
- GPTZero's later funding and user numbers, and WikiTok's post-launch status (investor interest, app versions), were not verified.
- The "went to zero" claim for fly.pieter.com could not be read (DEV fetch failed).
- No 2025-2026 example was found of a solo-built scam checker or safety tool going viral. That is an evidence gap, and possibly an open lane.
- No participation numbers were found for Google Jigsaw's 2019 phishing quiz, the closest "test your scam-spotting" precedent — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2019/01/22/google-jigsaw-phishing-emails/); [Vice](https://vice.com/en/article/google-jigsaw-phishing-quiz)

## Q3. Which of these problems have naturally shareable outputs (a result people post or forward)?

### Takeaway
Fraud and safety outputs are the most naturally shareable of the serious problems, because people already forward warnings into family group chats. A scam-check verdict card, a family safe-word invite and a "can you spot the scam?" quiz score all have a built-in reason to send to others. Health, legal, privacy and financial outputs are private and spread through press and word of mouth, not shares.

### Cited Findings
- WhatsApp and instant messaging are the primary scam channel in the UAE, so the scam arrives in the same place a warning would be forwarded — [WAM / GASA 2024](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/b6f93te-2024-state-scams-uae-survey-reveals-65-scam)
- The FBI's recommended defense against voice-clone scams is a family secret word agreed in advance. That requires coordinating with relatives, which makes it inherently multi-user — [Bitdefender on FBI PSA](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/blog/hotforsecurity/fbi-alarm-ai-scams)
- 46% of UK adults had never heard of voice-clone scams (Starling 2024, via aggregator), so awareness itself is a gap a shareable explainer can fill — [SQ Magazine](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/ai-voice-cloning-fraud-statistics/)
- Older adults bear the largest losses ($7.7B, +59%). They are often reached through younger relatives, who are the likely sharers — [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/fbi-ftc-report-2025-losses.html)
- Score-based "test yourself" formats spread at scale: Human or Not reached 1.5M+ users in a month, and Instafest's niche "Basic Score" and Wordle's grid both drove sharing — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20010v1); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/28/instafest-app-lets-you-create-your-own-festival-lineup-from-spotify); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2022/01/31/1077089945/nyt-wordle)
- A precedent for a scam-spotting quiz: Jigsaw's 8-question phishing quiz (2019) drew international press in several languages, but participation numbers were not found — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2019/01/22/google-jigsaw-phishing-emails/); [TecMundo](https://www.tecmundo.com.br/seguranca/138103-google-mostra-identificar-golpes-phishing-teste-didatico.htm); [Siècle Digital](https://siecledigital.fr/2019/01/23/google-quiz-hameconnage/)
- Utility tools with private outputs (Fight Health Insurance, Counterforce) spread through local and national press and news pegs rather than user posts — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/local/raleigh/2025/08/20/using-ai-to-fight-back-against-insurance-denials-counteforce); [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2024/12/16/how-to-fight-insurance-denials-luigi-mangione-unitedhealthcare-centene-medicaid-aca-outlook-healthcare-inc-newsletter/)

### Inferences
- **Shareability by problem** (H = high, M = medium, L = low):
  - Scam-message checker: **H**. Output is a branded "this is a scam — here's why" card or link, forwarded back into the family or neighborhood group where the scam circulated. Each forward is an acquisition event, and it is useful to the recipient, not just a brag.
  - Voice-clone family kit: **H**. Shareable as a "set up our family safe word" invite link that relatives must open, a k-factor built in by necessity, plus a printable or WhatsApp-able one-page explainer for parents and grandparents.
  - "Spot the scam / spot the AI voice or image" quiz: **H**. Score card ("I got 6/10 — can you beat me?") uses the Wordle/Instafest score mechanic and doubles as education. It could be the top of the funnel for the checker.
  - Job-posting scam or ghost-job checker: **M**. Shared in job-seeker communities and group chats ("don't apply to this one"), less in public feeds.
  - "Is this image/claim real?" checker: **M**. People reply to misinformation with a result link, but contested verdicts can backfire.
  - Subscription-waste finder: **M**. A "$X/year wasted" number is braggable or embarrassing, but uploading a bank statement suppresses use.
  - Document, lab-result, insurance-denial and data-broker tools: **L**. Outputs are private. Growth would rely on SEO, press and word of mouth.
- **Best-fit viral loop for the end user (a solo beginner, late 2026):** a free, no-signup, Arabic + English scam and voice-clone safety tool:
  1. A shareable quiz pulls people in.
  2. The checker turns each use into a forwardable verdict card in WhatsApp.
  3. The family safe-word invite brings in relatives.
  4. Launch is timed to a news peg (a new FTC or IC3 report, a viral deepfake incident, Ramadan or holiday shopping-scam season), via Hacker News or Reddit for the English side and Arabic social channels for the Arabic side.
  This combines the highest-scale problem, the clearest competitor gap (English-only incumbents), and the most natural sharing behavior.
- Design implications from the cases: put the URL and brand on the result image, make the share one tap (Web Share API or a WhatsApp deep link), keep it spoiler-free and privacy-safe (no personal data in the shared card), and cache repeated scam texts and URLs to cut LLM cost.

### Gaps
- No direct data was found on how often people forward scam warnings in WhatsApp groups, or on share rates for safety tools compared with entertainment tools. The forwarding claim is inferred from channel data, not measured.
- No case was found of a safety or scam tool with documented viral sharing numbers, so the predicted "H" shareability for safety outputs is untested by precedent.
